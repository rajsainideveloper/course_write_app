# Paper Folding & Cutting

> The geometry of symmetry: tracing the echoes of a single cut across multiple layers of folded paper. This is not about visualizing the fold; it is about understanding the symmetrical propagation of cuts across each crease line.

## Chapter 1: The Foundations of Paper Folding Logic

### Section 1 | What Is Paper Folding & Cutting?

Paper folding and cutting questions present a sequence of folds performed on a piece of paper, followed by one or more cuts. Your task is to determine the pattern of holes (or cuts) that appears when the paper is unfolded.

**The Core Principle**: Every fold creates a line of symmetry. When a cut is made through folded paper, the cut is replicated across each layer along the fold lines. The number of holes in the unfolded paper equals the number of layers penetrated by the cut, and their positions are determined by reflecting the cut across each crease line.

**The Cognitive Shift**: Do not try to physically imagine the paper unfolding in 3D. Instead, use the principle of reflection: each fold creates a mirror line. The cut pattern on each layer is the mirror image of the cut on the adjacent layer across that fold line. Trace the cut backward through the folds.

[alert type="danger"]
**The Layer Count Trap**: Students often forget that a fold creates multiple layers. A single cut through a paper folded in half creates *two* holes when unfolded. A cut through a paper folded in quarters creates *four* holes (one per layer). Always count the layers before determining the number of holes.
[/alert]

### Section 2 | The Reflection Principle

| Fold Direction | Crease Line | Effect on Cut Pattern |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Fold in half vertically | Vertical crease | Left half mirrors right half |
| Fold in half horizontally | Horizontal crease | Top half mirrors bottom half |
| Fold in quarters | Two creases | Both horizontal and vertical symmetry |

When a cut is made, each layer receives an identical cut. The cut on one layer is the mirror image of the cut on the folded layer. Unfolding reveals the cut repeated symmetrically across each crease.

## Chapter 2: Symmetry and Crease Lines

### Section 1 | The Fold-Cut Relationship

The relationship between folds and cuts is governed by symmetry:

1. **Each fold creates a line of symmetry** in the final pattern.
2. **A cut on one side of a fold** creates a mirror image on the other side.
3. **The number of holes doubles** for each layer of paper the cut penetrates.

**Example**: A paper is folded in half vertically. A circular cut is made near the folded edge.
- When unfolded, the cut appears as two circles: one on the left, one on the right, symmetric about the vertical crease.

### Section 2 | Tracing the Cut Backward

To determine the final pattern:
1. **Start from the final folded state** (the image given).
2. **Reflect the cut** across the last fold line to get the pattern on the previous layer.
3. **Repeat** for each fold in reverse order.
4. **The result** is the pattern on the fully unfolded paper.

[alert type="success"]
**The Reverse Unfolding Method**: Work backward from the folded cut to the unfolded pattern. At each step, reflect the existing cut pattern across the crease line that was folded. This is easier than trying to imagine the folding process forward.
[/alert]

| 1 (half) | 1 horizontal | Top-bottom symmetry | 2 |

## Chapter 3: Folded Paper (Half/Quarter)

### Section 1 | Single Fold (Half)

When a paper is folded in half, there are two layers. A cut made anywhere creates two holes: one on each half of the paper.

**Algorithm for Half Fold**:
1. Identify the crease line (vertical or horizontal).
2. Draw the cut on one half of the folded paper.
3. Reflect the cut across the crease line to the other half.
4. The final pattern is the combination of the original cut and its reflection.

**Example**: A paper is folded in half vertically. A cut is made near the top-left of the folded paper.
- In the folded state, the cut is on the left layer (which is actually the left half of the paper).
- When unfolded, the cut appears on the left half (original) and the right half (reflection).

### Section 2 | Double Fold (Quarter)

When a paper is folded twice (in quarters), there are four layers. A cut creates four holes: one in each quadrant.

**Algorithm for Quarter Fold**:
1. Identify both crease lines (vertical and horizontal).
2. Draw the cut on the folded paper.
3. Reflect across the vertical crease.
4. Reflect across the horizontal crease (both the original and the first reflection).
5. The final pattern has four symmetric copies.

[alert type="info"]
**The Quadrant Symmetry**: In a quarter-fold, the final pattern always has both vertical and horizontal symmetry. The four copies occupy the four quadrants of the paper.
[/alert]

[flashcards title="Fold Types and Layer Counts"]
| Fold Type | Number of Layers | Number of Creases | Symmetry Axes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| No fold | 1 | 0 | None |
| Half (vertical) | 2 | 1 | Vertical |
| Half (horizontal) | 2 | 1 | Horizontal |
| Quarter (vertical + horizontal) | 4 | 2 | Vertical and horizontal |
| Diagonal | 2 | 1 | Diagonal |
[/flashcards]

[example]
**Question:** A paper is folded in half vertically. A cut is made as shown (a triangle pointing left). Draw the unfolded pattern.
**Solution:**
Step 1: The cut is on the left half of the folded paper.
Step 2: Reflect the triangle across the vertical crease. The reflected triangle will point right.
Step 3: The unfolded paper will have a triangle pointing left on the left half and a triangle pointing right on the right half.
Step 4: Both triangles are symmetric about the vertical crease.
**Final Answer:** Two triangles, one pointing left and one pointing right, symmetric about the center.
[/example]

## Chapter 4: Cut Design and Unfolded Pattern

### Section 1 | Interpreting the Cut on the Folded Paper

The cut design is drawn on the folded paper as you would see it in the folded state. You must determine which part of the cut falls on which layer.

**The Overlap Problem**: When the paper is folded, multiple layers overlap. A cut on the folded paper cuts through all layers. In the unfolded pattern, the cut appears on each layer.

**Example**: A paper is folded in half horizontally. A cut is made along the folded edge.
- The cut is on the edge where the fold is.
- When unfolded, the cut appears as a slit across the center, with mirrored shapes on both halves.

### Section 2 | The Symmetrical Propagation

The key to solving cut design problems is recognizing that each cut creates a symmetrical pattern. The number of copies equals the number of layers.

**The Three-Step Process**:
1. **Count the layers**: Determine how many layers the cut penetrates.
2. **Identify the fold lines**: These are the axes of symmetry.
3. **Reflect the cut**: Create copies of the cut across each axis.

[alert type="warning"]
**The Shape Distortion**: The shape of the cut may appear distorted on the final unfolded paper if the cut was made at an angle to the fold. However, for most exam questions, the cut is made perpendicular to the paper surface, so the shape is preserved on each layer.
[/alert]

[flashcards title="Cut Propagation Rules"]
| Cut Location | Fold Type | Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Near center | Half fold | Two copies, one on each half |
| Near edge | Half fold | Two copies, one mirrored |
| Corner | Quarter fold | Four copies, one in each quadrant |
| Along fold | Half fold | Cut appears as a continuous slit |
[/flashcards]

[example]
**Question:** A square paper is folded in half vertically, then in half horizontally (quarter fold). A cut is made in the folded paper as shown (a small square in the center). What does the unfolded paper look like?
**Solution:**
Step 1: The folded paper is a quarter of the original size (lower-right quadrant).
Step 2: The cut is a square in the center of this folded quarter.
Step 3: Unfold horizontally: reflect the square across the horizontal crease. Now there are two squares: one in the lower-right, one in the upper-right.
Step 4: Unfold vertically: reflect both squares across the vertical crease. Now there are four squares: one in each quadrant.
Step 5: The final pattern is four identical squares, one in each quadrant, symmetric about both axes.
**Final Answer:** Four squares, one in each corner/quadrant of the paper.
[/example]

## Chapter 5: Finding the Final Figure

### Section 1 | The Reverse Engineering Approach

Given the cut design on the folded paper, determine the final figure after unfolding. This is the most common question type.

**The Step-by-Step Method**:
1. **Draw the folded paper** as a smaller rectangle representing the folded size.
2. **Mark the cut** on this folded rectangle.
3. **Unfold one step at a time**:
   - For each fold, reflect all existing cut marks across the fold line.
   - Add the reflected marks to the pattern.
4. **Continue until fully unfolded**.
5. **The final pattern** is the answer.

### Section 2 | The Symmetry Check

After you have the final pattern, verify it satisfies all the symmetries imposed by the folds. If the paper was folded in half vertically, the final pattern must be vertically symmetric. If it was folded in quarters, it must be both vertically and horizontally symmetric.

**The Trap**: If your final pattern violates a symmetry, you have made an error in your reflections. Go back and check.

[alert type="success"]
**The Visual Check**: Once you have the final figure, visually check it for the expected symmetries. This quick verification will catch most mistakes.
[/alert]

[flashcards title="Unfolding Quick Reference"]
| Fold Type | Final Symmetry | Check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Vertical half | Vertical symmetry | Left = Right mirror |
| Horizontal half | Horizontal symmetry | Top = Bottom mirror |
| Quarter | Both symmetries | All four quadrants symmetric |
| Diagonal | Diagonal symmetry | Mirrored across diagonal |
[/flashcards]

[example]
**Question:** A paper is folded in half horizontally. A cut is made in the folded paper as shown (a semicircle on the left side). What is the final unfolded pattern?
**Solution:**
Step 1: The folded paper has the cut on the left side of the folded piece.
Step 2: The horizontal fold means the bottom half is folded up over the top half. The cut on the left side of the folded paper cuts through both layers.
Step 3: When unfolded, the cut appears on the left side of the top half (original) and on the left side of the bottom half (reflection).
Step 4: The final pattern is two semicircles on the left side, one on the top half and one on the bottom half, symmetric about the horizontal crease.
**Final Answer:** Two semicircles on the left side, one above and one below the center horizontal line.
[/example]
