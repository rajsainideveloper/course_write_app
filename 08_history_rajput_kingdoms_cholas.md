# History: Rajput Kingdoms & Cholas

> From the battlefields of Tarain to the shores of Srivijaya—the warrior ethos of the Rajputs and the maritime might of the Cholas defined medieval South India.

## Chapter 1: The Rajput Kingdoms—The Warrior Clans

### Section 1 | The Rise of the Rajputs

The term *Rajput* (from *Raja-putra*—"son of a king") refers to the *warrior clans* that rose to prominence in *northern and western India* between the *6th and 12th centuries* CE. Their *origins* are debated, but the *examination* focuses on their *political* and *cultural* achievements.

**The Agnikula Myth:**

- According to *myth*, the Rajputs *originated* from a *sacrificial fire* (agni-kunda) on *Mount Abu*.
- The *legend* was *promoted* by the *Brahmins* to *legitimize* the Rajputs as *Kshatriyas* (they were *originally* of *mixed* origin).
- The *four* clans of the *Agnikula* are:
  1. **Pratiharas** (already discussed)
  2. **Chauhans**
  3. **Solankis** (also called *Chalukyas* of Gujarat)
  4. **Paramaras**

[alert type="warning"]
**Exam Trap:**
The *Agnikula* myth is *not* historically *accurate*—it is a *legend*. The exam tests the *association* of the *four* clans with the *fire-pit* myth. Do not treat it as *historical* fact—treat it as *cultural* tradition.
[/alert]

---

### Section 2 | The Chauhans—The Defenders of the North

The *Chauhans* (also called *Chahamanas*) ruled from *Ajmer* and *Delhi*. They were the *most powerful* Rajput clan in the *12th century*.

**Prithviraj Chauhan (≈1178–1192 CE):**

- Prithviraj Chauhan was the *greatest* Chauhan king—he *ruled* from *Ajmer* and *Delhi*.
- He *defeated* the *Chandela* king *Paramardi* (of Khajuraho) and *annexed* the *Bundelkhand* region.
- He *repelled* the *early* Turkish invasions—he *defeated* *Muhammad Ghori* at the *First Battle of Tarain* (1191 CE).

[alert type="success"]
**Important Fact:**
The *First Battle of Tarain* (1191 CE) was *won* by Prithviraj Chauhan. The *Second Battle of Tarain* (1192 CE) was *won* by *Muhammad Ghori*—and Prithviraj was *defeated* and *killed*.
[/alert]

**The Two Battles of Tarain:**

| Battle | Year | Victor | Consequence |
|--------|------|--------|-------------|
| First Battle of Tarain | 1191 CE | Prithviraj Chauhan | Ghori retreated |
| Second Battle of Tarain | 1192 CE | Muhammad Ghori | Prithviraj killed; Turkish rule in Delhi |

**The Legend of Prithviraj:**

- Prithviraj Chauhan is *romanticized* in *folk* literature—the *Prithviraj Raso* (composed by *Chand Bardai*) is a *poetic* account of his life.
- The *Raso* describes Prithviraj as a *great* warrior—it *exaggerates* his *achievements*.

[alert type="info"]
**Examiner's Focus:**
The *Prithviraj Raso* is a *literary* source—it is *not* a *historical* source. The exam tests the *distinction* between *historical* and *literary* sources. The *historical* details of Prithviraj's reign are *limited*—we rely on *inscriptions* and *Muslim* chronicles.
[/alert]

### Section 3 | The Solankis—The Patrons of Jainism

The *Solankis* (also called the *Chalukyas of Gujarat*) ruled *Gujarat* from the *10th to the 13th century* CE.

**Bhimdev I (≈1022–1064 CE):**

- Bhimdev I was the *most powerful* Solanki king—he *defeated* the *Paramara* king *Bhoja*.
- He *built* the *Sun Temple* at *Modhera*—a *masterpiece* of Solanki architecture.
- He *patronized* *Jainism*—the *Jain* temples at *Dilwara* (Mount Abu) were *built* during his *reign*.

**Kumarapala (≈1143–1174 CE):**

- Kumarapala was a *patron* of *Jainism*—he *converted* to Jainism under the *influence* of the *Jain* teacher *Hemachandra*.
- He *built* the *Jain* temples at *Patan* and *Somnath*.

[alert type="warning"]
**Exam Trap:**
The *Solankis* are *not* the same as the *Chalukyas of Badami*—the *Badami Chalukyas* ruled the *Deccan*; the *Solankis* ruled *Gujarat*. The exam tests this *geographic* distinction.
[/alert]

**The Dilwara Temples (Mount Abu):**

- The *Dilwara Temples* are *Jain* temples—they are *renowned* for their *marble* *sculpture*.
- They were *built* between the *11th and 13th centuries* CE—they *represent* the *peak* of *Solanki* architecture.

---

### Section 4 | The Paramaras—The Kings of Malwa

The *Paramaras* ruled *Malwa* (central India) from the *9th to the 14th century* CE.

**Raja Bhoja (≈1010–1055 CE):**

- Bhoja was the *greatest* Paramara king—he *ruled* from *Ujjain* and *Dhar*.
- He was a *patron* of *learning*—he *built* the *Bhojshala* (a *Sanskrit* college) in *Ujjain*.
- He *wrote* *many* works—the *Saraswati Kanthabharana* is a *treatise* on *poetics*.

**The Bhojshala:**

- The *Bhojshala* is a *Sanskrit* college—it *attracted* scholars from *all* over India.
- It was *destroyed* by the *Turks* in the *13th century*—but the *ruins* *still* exist.

[flashcards title="Paramara Dynasty Essentials"]
| Fact | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Capital | Ujjain, Dhar |
| Greatest king | Raja Bhoja (1010–1055 CE) |
| Patronage | Sanskrit learning |
| Bhojshala | Sanskrit college in Ujjain |
[/flashcards]

## Chapter 2: The Chola Empire—The Masters of the Sea

### Section 1 | The Rise of the Cholas

The Cholas were a *Tamil* dynasty—they *ruled* the *Kaveri* valley from the *9th to the 13th century* CE. They *emerged* as a *major* power after the *decline* of the *Pallavas*.

**The Founder: Vijayalaya (≈850–870 CE):**

- Vijayalaya was the *founder* of the *Chola* empire—he *captured* *Thanjavur* from the *Muttaraiyars*.
- He *built* a *temple* at *Thanjavur*—the *Nishumbhasudini* temple.

**The Great Chola Kings:**

| King | Reign | Key Achievement |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| Rajaraja I | 985–1014 CE | Built Brihadeshwara Temple; conquered Sri Lanka |
| Rajendra I | 1014–1044 CE | Naval expedition to Srivijaya; built Gangaikondacholapuram |
| Kulottunga I | 1070–1120 CE | United Chola and Vengi; patron of literature |

[alert type="success"]
**Important Fact:**
The Chola empire is *remarkable* for its *naval* power—they were the *first* Indian dynasty to *project* power across the *Bay of Bengal*. Rajendra I's *expedition* to *Srivijaya* (Sumatra) in ≈1025 CE is a *testament* to their *maritime* might.
[/alert]

---

### Section 2 | Rajaraja I—The Builder of the Brihadeshwara Temple

Rajaraja I (≈985–1014 CE) was the *greatest* Chola king—he *transformed* the Chola kingdom into an *empire*.

**The Conquests:**

- Rajaraja I *conquered* *Sri Lanka*—he *annexed* the *northern* part of the island.
- He *defeated* the *Cheras* and the *Pandyas*—he *unified* the *Tamil* region.
- He *subjugated* the *Gangas* of *Odisha* and the *Chalukyas* of *Kalyani*.

**The Brihadeshwara Temple (Thanjavur):**

- The *Brihadeshwara Temple* is *dedicated* to *Shiva*—it is *also* called the *Rajarajeshwara* temple.
- It is *built* of *granite*—the *tower* (vimana) is ≈66 m tall (the *tallest* in India).
- The *sanctum* houses a *huge* *Shiva* linga—≈4 m tall.
- The *walls* are *covered* with *sculptures*—the *Nataraja* (dancing Shiva) is the *most* famous.

[alert type="info"]
**Examiner's Focus:**
The *Brihadeshwara Temple* is a *UNESCO* World Heritage site—it is *one* of the *greatest* examples of *Dravidian* architecture. The exam often tests the *association*: Rajaraja I → Brihadeshwara Temple → Thanjavur.
[/alert]

**The Administration of Rajaraja I:**

- Rajaraja I *introduced* a *land survey*—he *measured* the *land* and *assessed* the *taxes*.
- He *built* *irrigation* tanks—he *improved* the *agricultural* productivity of the Kaveri valley.
- He *patronized* *Shaivism*—he *made* *endowments* to *temples*.

---

### Section 3 | Rajendra I—The Naval Conqueror

Rajendra I (≈1014–1044 CE) was the *son* of Rajaraja I—he *continued* his father's *expansionist* policy.

**The Naval Expedition to Srivijaya (≈1025 CE):**

- Rajendra I *sent* a *naval* fleet to *Srivijaya* (Sumatra)—he *conquered* the *kingdom* and *took* the *king* *prisoner*.
- The *expedition* was *motivated* by *trade*—the Cholas *wanted* to *control* the *Malacca* Strait (the *key* to the *spice* trade).
- The *expedition* *established* Chola *influence* in *Southeast Asia*.

**The Title: Gangaikondachola**

- Rajendra I *assumed* the title *Gangaikondachola* ("the Chola who took the Ganga")—he *defeated* the *Ganga* dynasty of Odisha and *marched* to the *Ganga*.
- He *built* a *new* capital at *Gangaikondacholapuram*—he *built* a *temple* there (the *Brihadeshwara Temple* of the north).

**The Gangaikondacholapuram Temple:**

- The *temple* is *smaller* than the *Brihadeshwara* Temple—but it is *architecturally* *refined*.
- It has a *vimana* of ≈50 m—it is *dedicated* to *Shiva*.

## Chapter 3: Chola Administration—The System of Governance

### Section 1 | The Central Administration

The Chola king was the *head* of the *state*—he *ruled* with the *advice* of his *council*.

**The King:**

- The Chola king was *absolute*—he *controlled* the *army*, the *treasury*, and the *judiciary*.
- He *took* *titles* that *emphasized* his *divine* status—*Mahaarajadhiraja* and *Parakesari*.

**The Council of Ministers:**

- The Chola king *appointed* *ministers*—they *administered* the *empire*.
- The *council* *included* the *Chief Minister* (Pradhan), the *Commander-in-Chief* (Senapati), and the *Treasurer* (Bhandarik).

---

### Section 2 | The Provincial Administration

The Chola empire was *divided* into *provinces*—the *Mandalam* was the *largest* administrative unit.

**The Administrative Hierarchy:**

| Level | Tamil Term | Function |
|-------|------------|----------|
| Province | Mandalam | Governor (Viceroy) |
| Sub-province | Kottam | Regional officer |
| District | Nadu | Assembly of villages |
| Village | Ur (rural) / Nagaram (urban) | Local assembly |

**The Local Self-Government:**

- The Cholas are *famous* for their *local* self-government—the *village* assemblies were *democratic*.
- The *Sabha* was the *assembly* of *Brahmins*—it *managed* the *temple* lands.
- The *Ur* was the *assembly* of *non-Brahmins*—it *managed* the *common* lands.
- The *Nagaram* was the *assembly* of *merchants*—it *managed* the *trade* affairs.

[alert type="warning"]
**Exam Trap:**
The *Sabha*, *Ur*, and *Nagaram* are *often* confused. The *Sabha* is *Brahmin*; the *Ur* is *non-Brahmin*; the *Nagaram* is *merchant*. The exam tests this *distinction*—memorize the *three* assemblies and their *constituencies*.
[/alert]

**The Land Revenue System:**

- The Cholas *collected* taxes on *land*—the tax rate was *one-sixth* of the produce.
- The *taxes* were *collected* in *kind* (grain) or *cash* (coins).
- The *temple* lands were *tax-exempt*—the *Brahmins* and *temples* were *given* grants of land.

---

### Section 3 | The Chola Military and Navy

**The Army:**

- The Chola army had *infantry*, *cavalry*, and *war elephants*.
- The *warriors* were *feudal* lords—they *served* the king in *exchange* for *land* grants.

**The Navy:**

- The Chola navy was the *most powerful* in *South Asia*—it *controlled* the *Bay of Bengal*.
- The *ships* were *built* of *teak* and *coir*—they *sailed* to *Sumatra*, *Java*, and *Malay*.

## Chapter 4: Chola Art and Architecture—The Dravidian Masterpieces

### Section 1 | The Brihadeshwara Temple (Thanjavur)

The *Brihadeshwara Temple* is the *crowning* achievement of Chola architecture—it is *one* of the *greatest* examples of *Dravidian* temple architecture.

**The Architectural Features:**

- *Vimana*: The *tower* is ≈66 m tall—it is *built* of *interlocking* stones (without *mortar*).
- *Gopuram*: The *gateway* tower is *smaller* than the *vimana*—the *Cholas* *emphasized* the *vimana* (unlike the *later* Nayakas).
- *Sculptures*: The *walls* are *carved* with *scenes* from the *Shiva Purana*—the *Nataraja* (dancing Shiva) is the *most* famous.

**The Inscriptions:**

- The *temple* has *over* 100 *inscriptions*—they *record* the *endowments* of Rajaraja I.
- The *inscriptions* describe the *donation* of *gold*, *silver*, and *land* to the *temple*.

---

### Section 2 | The Gangaikondacholapuram Temple

The *Gangaikondacholapuram Temple* was *built* by Rajendra I—it *commemorates* his *victory* over the *Gangas*.

**The Features:**

- The *vimana* is ≈50 m tall—it is *smaller* than the *Brihadeshwara* but *architecturally* *refined*.
- The *sanctum* houses a *Shiva* linga—the *walls* have *sculptures* of *Shiva* and *Vishnu*.

---

### Section 3 | The Darasuram Temple

The *Darasuram Temple* (≈1150 CE) was *built* by Rajaraja II—it is *one* of the *later* Chola temples.

**The Features:**

- The *vimana* is *decorated* with *sculptures* of *dancers* and *musicians*.
- The *temple* *marks* the *transition* from *early* Chola to *late* Chola architecture.

---

### Section 4 | Chola Bronze Sculptures

The Cholas are *renowned* for their *bronze* sculptures—they *used* the *lost-wax* technique.

**The Nataraja (Dancing Shiva):**

- The *Nataraja* is the *most* famous Chola bronze—it *depicts* Shiva *dancing* in the *Ananda Tandava* (the Dance of Bliss).
- The *iconography*:
  - *Four arms*—the *right* hand *holds* the *damaru* (drum); the *left* hand *holds* the *fire*.
  - *One leg* is *raised*—it *tramples* the *dwarf* (Apasmara).
  - The *dance* *symbolizes* the *cycle* of *creation*, *preservation*, and *destruction*.

[alert type="success"]
**Important Fact:**
The *Nataraja* is *not* just a sculpture—it is a *philosophical* statement. Shiva's dance is the *cosmic* dance—it *represents* the *interplay* of *matter* and *consciousness*. The exam often tests the *symbolism* of the Nataraja.
[/alert]

|----------|------|--------------|
| Brihadeshwara Temple | Rajaraja I | Tallest vimana; UNESCO site |
| Gangaikondacholapuram | Rajendra I | Commemorated Gangetic victory |
| Darasuram Temple | Rajaraja II | Later Chola architecture |
| Nataraja Bronze | Chola period | Dancing Shiva; lost-wax technique |
[/flashcards]

## Chapter 5: The Cheras and Pandyas—The Southern Neighbors

### Section 1 | The Cheras—The Ancient Traders

The Cheras *ruled* *Kerala* from the *1st millennium CE*—they are *mentioned* in the *Sangam* literature.

**The Chera Kings:**

- The *early* Cheras *ruled* from *Vanji* (also called *Karur*).
- They *traded* with the *Romans*—Roman *coins* have been *found* in *Kerala*.
- They *exported* *pepper*, *spices*, and *ivory*.

**The Tirukkural:**

- The *Tirukkural* is a *Tamil* text by *Tiruvalluvar*—it is a *collection* of *couplets* on *ethics*, *politics*, and *love*.
- It is *traditionally* *associated* with the *Sangam* period—it is *one* of the *greatest* works of Tamil literature.

---

### Section 2 | The Pandyas—The Masters of the Pearl

The Pandyas *ruled* *Madurai* from the *1st millennium CE*—they are *mentioned* in *Greek* and *Roman* texts.

**The Pandya Kings:**

- The *early* Pandyas *ruled* from *Madurai*—they *controlled* the *pearl* fisheries.
- They *traded* with *Rome*, *Southeast Asia*, and *China*.
- They *patronized* Tamil literature—the *Sangam* assemblies were *held* under their *patronage*.

**The Later Pandyas:**

- The *later* Pandyas (≈10th–14th centuries CE) *revived* the *kingdom* after the *Chola* *decline*.
- They *built* the *Meenakshi* Temple at *Madurai*—a *UNESCO* site.

[flashcards title="Cheras and Pandyas"]
| Dynasty | Capital | Trade | Key Text |
|---------|---------|-------|----------|
| Chera | Vanji | Romans (pepper, spices) | Tirukkural |
| Pandya | Madurai | Pearls, maritime trade | Sangam literature |
[/flashcards]

## Chapter 6: The Sangam Era—The Golden Age of Tamil Literature

### Section 1 | The Sangam Assemblies

The *Sangam* period (≈300 BCE–300 CE) was the *greatest* age of Tamil literature—the *term* "Sangam" means *assembly*.

**The Three Sangams:**

| Sangam | Location | Chair | Period (traditional) |
|--------|----------|-------|----------------------|
| First Sangam | Then Madurai | Agastya | ≈4,000 BCE (mythical) |
| Second Sangam | Kapatapuram | Tolkappiyar | ≈2,000 BCE (mythical) |
| Third Sangam | Madurai | Nakkirar | ≈300 BCE–300 CE (historical) |

[alert type="warning"]
**Exam Trap:**
The *First* and *Second* Sangams are *mythical*—the *Third* Sangam is the *only* historically *attested* Sangam. The exam tests this *distinction*—do not treat the *early* Sangams as *historical* facts.
[/alert]

---

### Section 2 | The Sangam Literature

**The Tolkappiyam:**

- The *Tolkappiyam* is the *earliest* Tamil grammar—it *dates* to the *Sangam* period.
- It *describes* the *phonetics*, *morphology*, and *syntax* of Tamil.

**The Ettuthogai (Eight Anthologies):**

- The *Ettuthogai* is a *collection* of *eight* works—they *contain* *over* 2,000 *poems*.
- The *most* famous is the *Kurinji* (love poems) and the *Palai* (the desert theme).

**The Pattupattu (Ten Idylls):**

- The *Pattupattu* is a *collection* of *ten* long poems—they *describe* the *landscape*, *economy*, and *society* of the *Sangam* period.

---

### Section 3 | The Themes of Sangam Literature

**The Aham (Love) and Puram (War):**

- Sangam poetry is *divided* into *two* categories:
  - *Aham*: Poems about *love*, *separation*, and *longing*.
  - *Puram*: Poems about *war*, *heroism*, and *royal* *patronage*.

**The Five Landscapes (Tinai):**

| Landscape | Tamil Term | Symbolism |
|-----------|------------|-----------|
| Mountain | Kurinji | Union of lovers |
| Forest | Mullai | Waiting |
| Agricultural | Marutam | Sulking |
| Coastal | Neithal | Separation |
| Desert | Palai | Difficulty |

[alert type="info"]
**Examiner's Focus:**
The *five landscapes* are a *unique* feature of Sangam literature—the exam often tests the *association* of *each* landscape with *emotion*. Memorize the *Tinai* and their *symbolism*.
[/alert]

[flashcards title="Sangam Literature"]
| Work | Type | Significance |
|------|------|--------------|
| Tolkappiyam | Grammar | Earliest Tamil text |
| Ettuthogai | 8 anthologies | Love and war poetry |
| Pattupattu | 10 idylls | Long narrative poems |
| Tirukkural | Couplets | Ethics, politics, love |
[/flashcards]

## Chapter 7: Synthesis—The Legacy of the Rajputs and Cholas

### Section 1 | The Conceptual Takeaway

1. **Rajputs**: The *warrior ethos*—the *defense* of *northern* India against *Turkish* invasions.

2. **Cholas**: The *maritime* empire—the *projection* of Indian *power* across the *Bay of Bengal*.

3. **Sangam Literature**: The *foundation* of Tamil *culture*—the *poetry* that *defines* the *classical* period.

### Section 2 | The Examination Mindset

1. **Identify the "Which":** Is the question asking about a *king*, a *temple*, a *text*, or a *battle*? Your mental checklist: *Person*, *Place*, *Text*, *Event*.

2. **Identify the "When":** The *Rajput* period is *later* (10th–12th cent.); the *Chola* period *overlaps* (9th–13th cent.). The *Sangam* period is *earlier* (300 BCE–300 CE). Use the *monuments* as *chronological anchors*: Brihadeshwara Temple (≈1000 CE), Dilwara Temples (≈12th cent.).

3. **Identify the "Where":** The *regions* are *critical*:
   - Chauhan: *North* (Ajmer, Delhi)
   - Solanki: *West* (Gujarat)
   - Paramara: *Central* (Malwa)
   - Chola: *South-east* (Thanjavur)
   - Sangam: *South* (Madurai)

4. **Avoid the "Isolationist Trap":** The Cholas *traded* with *Southeast Asia*; the Cheras *traded* with *Rome*; the Rajputs *fought* the *Turks*. This is a *period* of *international* *exchange*.
