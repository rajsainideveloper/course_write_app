# Mirror Image

> The geometry of reflection: decoding the lateral inversion that transforms objects across the looking glass. This is not about recognizing flipped shapes; it is about systematically reconstructing the mirror's transformation of letters, numbers, words, and clocks.

## Chapter 1: The Foundations of Mirror Logic

### Section 1 | What Is a Mirror Image?

A mirror image is the result of reflecting an object across a plane. In the context of competitive exams, the mirror is usually placed vertically (to the left or right of the object) or horizontally (above or below). The most common type is the **vertical mirror**, which causes lateral inversion—left and right swap positions.

**The Core Principle**: Reflection across a vertical axis reverses the order of elements (left becomes right) and also reverses individual characters that are not symmetrical. The transformation is point-by-point: each point moves to a position at the same distance from the mirror on the opposite side.

**The Cognitive Shift**: When solving mirror image questions, do not try to "rotate" the object in your mind. Instead, trace the path of each element across the mirror line. For letters and numbers, memorize the mirror images of common characters.

[alert type="danger"]
**The Left-Right Reversal**: In a vertical mirror, the order of characters is reversed. The first character on the left becomes the last character on the right. This is the most common source of errors—students often reverse individual characters but forget to reverse the order.
[/alert]

### Section 2 | The Reflection Axis

| Mirror Type | Axis | Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Vertical (Left/Right) | Vertical line | Lateral inversion: left ↔ right |
| Horizontal (Top/Bottom) | Horizontal line | Top ↔ bottom inversion |
| Clock Mirror | Vertical axis through 6-12 | Reverses clock face; must interpret time |

## Chapter 2: Horizontal and Vertical Mirror

### Section 1 | Lateral Inversion (Vertical Mirror)

In a vertical mirror, every element is reflected across a vertical axis. The order of characters reverses, and each character is laterally inverted.

**Example**:
- Object: `C A T`
- Vertical mirror: `T A C` (order reversed) with each letter inverted.
- After inversion: `T` (inverted), `A` (unchanged, symmetric), `C` (inverted).
- Final: `T A C` where C and T are flipped. So the mirror image of `CAT` is `TAC` with appropriate letter flips.

**The Mirror Line**: The mirror is usually placed at the end of the word. If the mirror is on the left, the word is reflected to the right. If on the right, reflected to the left. The effect on order is the same.

### Section 2 | The Alphabet of Symmetry

Some letters and numbers are **symmetrical**—they look the same in a mirror (or nearly so). Memorize these:

- **Vertically Symmetric (Vertical Mirror)**: `A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y`
- **Horizontally Symmetric (Horizontal Mirror)**: `B, C, D, E, H, I, K, O, X`
- **Both Vertical and Horizontal Symmetric**: `H, I, O, X`
- **Numbers in Vertical Mirror**: `0, 1, 8` (sometimes 3 and 2 are tricky; most exams use standard font)
- **Numbers with Lateral Inversion**: `2` appears as `S`-like, `3` appears as `Ɛ`, `4` as `ꟼ`, `5` as `S`, `6` and `9` swap.

[alert type="success"]
**The Symmetry Shortcut**: In mirror image questions, focus on the characters that are *not* symmetrical. The symmetrical ones will look the same, making the transformation easier to spot.
[/alert]

[example]
**Question:** What is the mirror image of the word `PERSON` when the mirror is placed on the right side?
**Solution:**
Step 1: The order reverses: `N O S R E P`.
Step 2: Invert each character:
- N (symmetrical?) N is vertically symmetric (looks same in mirror).
- O (symmetrical).
- S (not symmetrical; in standard font, S becomes inverted S, like Ƨ).
- R (not symmetrical; becomes ᴚ).
- E (not symmetrical; becomes Ǝ).
- P (not symmetrical; becomes Ԁ).
Step 3: Combine: `N O Ƨ ᴚ Ǝ Ԁ`.
Step 4: Depending on the font, the answer is the reversed sequence with appropriate flips.
**Final Answer:** `N O S R E P` with each non-symmetric letter flipped.
[/example]

## Chapter 3: Clock Mirror Image

### Section 1 | The Clock Reflection Problem

Mirror images of a clock are a special case. When a clock is reflected in a vertical mirror, the positions of the hour and minute hands appear inverted. The key is to interpret the reflected time and determine the actual time.

**The Core Formula**:
=Actual Time = 11:60 - Reflected Time=
(Or: `Actual Time = 12:00 - Reflected Time` in a 12-hour format)

**Example**: If the reflected clock shows 4:30, what is the actual time?
- Actual Time = 11:60 - 4:30 = 7:30.
- Check: 12:00 - 4:30 = 7:30.

### Section 2 | The Mirror Time Logic

The mirror reverses the clock face, but the hands move. The reflected time is the time you see in the mirror. The actual time is the symmetric opposite.

**The Algorithm**:
1. Read the time shown in the mirror.
2. Subtract this time from 11:60 (or 12:00).
3. The result is the actual time.

**The Trap**: Some students try to visualize the reflection and read the clock. This is time-consuming and error-prone. The formula is much faster.

[alert type="info"]
**The "11:60" Method**: `11:60` is the same as `12:00`, but it's easier to subtract because you can treat hours and minutes separately. Subtract the minutes from 60, the hours from 11 (and borrow if needed).
[/alert]

[flashcards title="Clock Mirror Formula"]
| Given | Formula | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Reflected Time (R) | Actual = 11:60 - R | R = 4:30 → 11:60 - 4:30 = 7:30 |
| Reflected Time (R) | Actual = 12:00 - R | R = 4:30 → 12:00 - 4:30 = 7:30 |
[/flashcards]

[example]
**Question:** The mirror image of a clock shows 2:15. What is the actual time?
**Solution:**
Step 1: Use the formula: Actual = 11:60 - 2:15.
Step 2: 11:60 - 2:15 = (11-2):(60-15) = 9:45.
Step 3: Check: 12:00 - 2:15 = 9:45.
**Final Answer:** 9:45
[/example]
