# Statement & Assumptions
> The architecture of unspoken premises: excavating the hidden beliefs that must be true for any statement to make sense. This is not about what the statement says; it is about what the statement *presupposes*.

## Chapter 1: The Foundations of Statement-Assumption Logic

### Section 1 | What Is an Assumption?

An assumption is an unstated belief or proposition that must be accepted as true for the given statement to be valid, meaningful, or logically coherent. In Statement-Assumption questions, you are given a statement (often a proposal, advertisement, or observation) and asked to identify which assumptions are *implicit* in it.

**The Core Principle**: An assumption is not the same as an inference. An inference is a conclusion drawn *from* the statement. An assumption is a premise that must be true *for* the statement to hold. Think of assumptions as the foundation upon which the statement is built.

**The Cognitive Shift**: When you read a statement, constantly ask: "What must I believe to be true for this statement to make sense?" Your answer to that question is the assumption.

[alert type="danger"]
**The Inference Trap**: Students often confuse assumptions with inferences. If the statement is "The ground is wet," an inference is "It rained." An assumption is "The ground can be wet" or "I am capable of observing wetness." The assumption is more foundational and less directly derived from the statement.
[/alert]

### Section 2 | The Implicit-Explicit Spectrum

- **Explicit Assumptions**: These are directly stated or obviously implied. They are usually the intended answers in easy questions.
- **Implicit Assumptions**: These are deeply embedded and not immediately obvious. They often reflect the worldview or context of the speaker.

**Example**:
- Statement: "Please fasten your seatbelt before the flight takes off."
- Explicit Assumption: "Seatbelts exist on this flight."
- Implicit Assumption: "Passengers value their safety" or "The flight is about to take off."

## Chapter 2: Implicit and Explicit Assumptions

### Section 1 | The Implicit Assumption Framework

Implicit assumptions are the hidden premises that are *taken for granted*. They are not stated but are necessary for the statement to be understood as intended.

**The Three Layers of Implicit Assumptions**:

1. **Existential Assumptions**: The things mentioned in the statement actually exist.
   - Statement: "The CEO's new strategy will save the company."
   - Assumption: "The CEO exists" and "The company exists."

2. **Contextual Assumptions**: The situation described is as the speaker believes it to be.
   - Statement: "The manager should fire the employee."
   - Assumption: "The manager has the authority to fire employees."

3. **Normative Assumptions**: The speaker's values or expectations about how things should be.
   - Statement: "You should vote in the election."
   - Assumption: "Voting is a civic duty" or "One vote matters."

### Section 2 | The Validation Test

To determine if an assumption is implicit, apply the **Negation Test**:
- Negate the assumption (turn it into its opposite).
- If the negation makes the statement unreasonable, contradictory, or nonsensical, the assumption is implicit.
- If the statement still makes sense without the assumption, it is not implicit.

**Example**:
- Statement: "Please close the door."
- Assumption: "The door is open."
- Negation: "The door is closed." If the door is already closed, the statement "Please close the door" is unreasonable. Therefore, the assumption is implicit.

[alert type="success"]
**The Negation Test**: This is the single most powerful tool for Statement-Assumption questions. Always apply it when you are unsure whether a proposition is truly an assumption.
[/alert]

[flashcards title="Assumption Identification Rules"]
| Rule | Explanation | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Existence | The statement presumes its subjects exist | "The king is wise" assumes a king exists |
| Context | The statement presumes the described situation | "Take the next left" assumes you are driving |
| Normative | The statement presumes values or expectations | "Don't litter" assumes littering is undesirable |
| Negation Test | If negating destroys the statement, it's an assumption | "Close the door" assumes it is open |
[/flashcards]

## Chapter 3: Direct and Indirect Assumptions

### Section 1 | The Direct Assumption

A direct assumption is one that is immediately necessary for the statement to make sense. It is close to the surface and can be inferred with minimal reasoning.

**Example**:
- Statement: "Children should not watch this movie."
- Direct Assumption: "The movie is inappropriate for children" or "Children can watch movies."

**Characteristics**:
- Directly supports the statement.
- Usually involves the primary subject or action.
- Easy to identify.

### Section 2 | The Indirect Assumption

An indirect assumption is one that is not immediately necessary but reflects a broader belief or context that supports the statement indirectly. It often involves secondary actors, enabling conditions, or background knowledge.

**Example**:
- Statement: "Children should not watch this movie."
- Indirect Assumption: "Parents have control over what children watch" or "Children have no ability to choose appropriate content."

**The Trap**: Exams often include a direct assumption as a distractor and the indirect assumption as the intended answer, especially if the question asks for "the most important assumption" or "the underlying assumption."

[alert type="info"]
**The Hierarchy of Assumptions**: Direct assumptions are about the *what* (the immediate action). Indirect assumptions are about the *how* and *why* (the enabling conditions). The most "implicit" assumptions are usually indirect.
[/alert]

[flashcards title="Direct vs. Indirect Assumptions"]
| Characteristic | Direct Assumption | Indirect Assumption |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Proximity | Close to the statement | Further from the statement |
| Necessity | Immediately required | Required for full understanding |
| Example | "The movie is inappropriate" | "Parents can enforce rules" |
| Detection | Easy | Requires deeper analysis |
[/flashcards]

[example]
**Question:** Statement: "To reduce traffic congestion, the city should build more flyovers." Which of the following is an implicit assumption?
**Solution:**
Step 1: Identify direct assumptions: "Traffic congestion exists." "Flyovers reduce congestion." "The city has the resources to build flyovers."
Step 2: Identify indirect assumptions: "Building more flyovers will not increase traffic in the long run (induced demand)." "The city's roads can support flyover construction." "There is no better alternative."
Step 3: Apply the negation test: If "flyovers do not reduce congestion" were true, the statement would be absurd. So this is an assumption. If "traffic congestion exists" is false, the statement is also absurd. So this is also an assumption.
Step 4: The question asks for "an implicit assumption." The most universally applicable is "Flyovers reduce traffic congestion" or "Traffic congestion is a problem."
**Final Answer:** "Flyovers help reduce traffic congestion" is an implicit assumption.
[/example]

[quiz title="Statement & Assumptions Mastery Quiz"]
Q: Statement: "You should drink at least 8 glasses of water every day." Which is an implicit assumption?
- A) Water is healthy (Correct: The statement presumes water is beneficial for health. This is a direct assumption.)
- B) Everyone has access to clean water (This is an indirect assumption but less immediate than A.)
- C) The speaker is a doctor
- D) 8 glasses is a precise recommendation

Q: Statement: "This medicine should be taken after meals." Which is NOT an assumption?
- A) The medicine exists
- B) The patient eats meals (Correct: The statement implies the patient may need medicine, but it doesn't assume they eat meals? Actually, if the patient doesn't eat meals, taking after meals is impossible. So it is an assumption. Let's check: The statement presumes the patient eats meals. So B is an assumption. What is NOT an assumption? "The medicine is effective" is not an assumption; it is a claim not necessarily implied. So the answer is "The medicine is effective.")
- C) The patient has a stomach
- D) The patient can swallow pills

Q: Statement: "All employees must attend the mandatory training session." Which is an implicit assumption?
- A) The training session exists (Correct: Direct assumption.)
- B) Employees value professional development (Indirect assumption, but not necessary for the statement to make sense.)
- C) The training session will be effective (Not necessary.)
- D) The company has a training room (Indirect but less likely.)

Q: Statement: "It is better to walk than to drive in the city." Which is an implicit assumption?
- A) Walking is faster than driving (This is not necessarily an assumption; it might be a conclusion.)
- B) The city has sidewalks (Correct: For walking to be possible and better, sidewalks must exist. This is an implicit assumption.)
- C) The city is congested
- D) Driving is expensive

Q: Statement: "The government should ban single-use plastics." Which is an implicit assumption?
- A) Single-use plastics are harmful to the environment (Correct: Direct assumption. If they are not harmful, the ban makes no sense.)
- B) The government has the power to ban plastics (Indirect assumption but also necessary.)
- C) The people will comply with the ban (Indirect but not strictly necessary.)
- D) There are alternatives to single-use plastics (This is also necessary for the ban to be reasonable, but less direct than A.)
[/quiz]
