# Figure Series (Non-Verbal)

> The choreography of shapes: decoding the sequential transformation that governs a visual progression. This is not about recognizing individual figures; it is about identifying the rule that links each frame to the next.

## Chapter 1: The Foundations of Visual Pattern Steps

### Section 1 | What Is a Figure Series?

A figure series presents a sequence of figures (usually 4 to 6) that follow a consistent transformation rule. Your task is to identify the rule and select the figure that continues the sequence or fills a missing position.

**The Core Principle**: Every figure in the series is generated by applying a deterministic operation to the previous figure. The operation may involve rotation, movement, addition, deletion, replacement, or a combination of these.

**The Cognitive Shift**: When analyzing a figure series, do not look at the figures as static images. Instead, track the *change* from one figure to the next. The pattern is always in the transition.

[alert type="danger"]
**The Frame Confusion**: Students often try to memorize the figures rather than tracking the changes. The key is to identify what moves, rotates, or changes between consecutive frames. The figures themselves are secondary; the transitions are primary.
[/alert]

### Section 2 | The Change Tracking Protocol

1. **Compare Figure 1 and Figure 2**: What changed? Count the changes.
2. **Compare Figure 2 and Figure 3**: Does the same change occur?
3. **Compare Figure 3 and Figure 4**: Is the change consistent?
4. **Identify the rule**: The consistent change across all steps is the rule.
5. **Apply the rule**: Extend the series to find the next figure.

## Chapter 2: Rotation Series

### Section 1 | Clockwise and Anticlockwise Rotation

In a rotation series, one or more elements rotate by a fixed angle at each step. The rotation can be:
- **Clockwise (CW)**: Rotating to the right.
- **Anticlockwise (ACW)**: Rotating to the left.

**Common Rotation Angles**:
- 45°, 90°, 135°, 180°

**Example**: A simple arrow pointing up. At each step, it rotates 90° clockwise: Up → Right → Down → Left → Up.

### Section 2 | Complex Rotation

Sometimes, different elements rotate at different rates, or the entire figure rotates while individual elements also rotate.

**The Trap**: Some rotations are not of the entire figure but of individual components within the figure. Track each component separately.

**Example**: A clock face with two hands. The hour hand moves 30° each step, while the minute hand moves 60° each step.

[alert type="success"]
**The Center of Rotation**: Always identify the center point about which the rotation occurs. In most figures, it is the geometric center. If the figure is not symmetric, the center may be a specific vertex or intersection point.
[/alert]

[flashcards title="Rotation Rules"]
| Rule | Description | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Clockwise (CW) | Rotates right | Arrow up → Arrow right |
| Anticlockwise (ACW) | Rotates left | Arrow up → Arrow left |
| 90° CW | Quarter turn right | Square with dot top → Square with dot right |
| 180° | Half turn | Triangle up → Triangle down |
| 45° CW | Eighth turn | Pentagon with vertex top → Vertex top-right |
[/flashcards]

[example]
**Question:** A series shows a triangle rotating. Figure 1: triangle pointing up. Figure 2: triangle pointing right. Figure 3: triangle pointing down. Figure 4: ?
**Solution:**
Step 1: Track the rotation: Up → Right → Down.
Step 2: The rotation is 90° clockwise each step.
Step 3: Next rotation: Down → Left (90° CW from down is left).
**Final Answer:** Triangle pointing left.
[/example]

## Chapter 3: Addition and Deletion of Elements

### Section 1 | Progressive Addition

In an addition series, elements are added to the figure at each step. The addition may follow a specific pattern (e.g., adding one line, one shape, or one dot).

**Example**: Figure 1: a circle. Figure 2: circle with a dot inside. Figure 3: circle with two dots. Figure 4: circle with three dots. The number of dots increases by 1 each step.

### Section 2 | Progressive Deletion

In a deletion series, elements are removed from the figure at each step. The deletion may follow a sequence (e.g., removing from the outside inward).

**Example**: Figure 1: a square with four small squares inside. Figure 2: three squares. Figure 3: two squares. Figure 4: one square. The count decreases by 1 each step.

**The Trap**: Addition and deletion can happen simultaneously—some elements are added while others are removed. Track both categories separately.

[alert type="info"]
**The Counting Rule**: Count the number of elements in each figure. The sequence of counts often reveals the rule (e.g., 1, 2, 3, 4 or 4, 3, 2, 1).
[/alert]

[flashcards title="Addition/Deletion Rules"]
| Type | Description | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Addition | Elements added at each step | 1 dot, 2 dots, 3 dots, 4 dots |
| Deletion | Elements removed at each step | 4 lines, 3 lines, 2 lines, 1 line |
| Alternating | Add, then delete, then add | 1, 2, 1, 2 |
| Patterned Addition | Added elements follow a sequence | Add a line at the top, then bottom, then left |
[/flashcards]

## Chapter 4: Movement Series

### Section 1 | Linear Movement

In a movement series, elements shift position within the figure at each step. The movement can be horizontal (left/right), vertical (up/down), or diagonal.

**Example**: A dot moves within a square: top-left → top-right → bottom-right → bottom-left → top-left.

### Section 2 | Multiple Element Movement

When multiple elements are moving, each may follow its own path, or they may all move in the same direction.

**The Trap**: Sometimes elements appear to move but are actually being replaced by new elements in new positions. Track the identity of each element if possible; if not, track the position pattern.

[alert type="success"]
**The Position Grid**: For movement series, draw a grid of possible positions (e.g., 3×3) and track the position of each element. This makes the movement rule immediately apparent.
[/alert]

[flashcards title="Movement Rules"]
| Movement Type | Description | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Horizontal | Moves left or right | Dot at left → Dot at right |
| Vertical | Moves up or down | Dot at top → Dot at bottom |
| Diagonal | Moves along a diagonal | Dot at top-left → Dot at bottom-right |
| Circular | Moves around a center | Dot moves around a circle |
| Spiral | Moves in a spiral pattern | Dot moves inward |
[/flashcards]

## Chapter 5: Replacement Series

### Section 1 | Shape Replacement

In a replacement series, one shape is replaced by another shape at each step. The replacement may follow a cycle (e.g., circle → square → triangle → circle).

**Example**: Figure 1: circle. Figure 2: square. Figure 3: triangle. Figure 4: circle. The shapes cycle through three forms.

### Section 2 | Positional Replacement

Replacement can also occur in specific positions. For example, in a 3×3 grid, the shape in the top-left position changes, then the top-middle, then the top-right.

**The Trap**: Replacement and movement can appear similar. If a shape disappears and a new shape appears, it is replacement. If the same shape moves, it is movement.

[flashcards title="Replacement Rules"]
| Type | Description | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Shape Cycle | Shapes cycle in a fixed order | Circle → Square → Triangle |
| Position Cycle | Shapes change position in sequence | Top-left changes, then top-middle |
| Dual Replacement | Two shapes replace each other | Circle and square swap |
[/flashcards]

- C) Circle (Correct: The sequence is circle, square, triangle, circle, so the next is circle.)
