# Figure Completion

> The art of visual closure: inferring the missing fragment that completes a pattern or structure. This is not about artistic intuition; it is about systematic analysis of symmetry, rotation, and relational logic.

## Chapter 1: The Foundations of Figure Completion

### Section 1 | What Is Figure Completion?

Figure completion questions present an incomplete figure—often a square, circle, or grid with one part missing—and ask you to select the option that correctly completes it. The completion must make the figure logically consistent, often by satisfying symmetry, pattern continuation, or relational rules from analogous figures.

**The Core Principle**: Figure completion questions test your ability to identify the underlying rule governing the figure's structure. Once the rule is understood, the missing part is the one that extends the rule consistently.

**The Cognitive Shift**: Treat the incomplete figure as a puzzle with hidden constraints. Every line, shape, and orientation is there for a reason. The missing piece is the one that satisfies all constraints.

[alert type="danger"]
**The Gestalt Trap**: Students often rely on intuition or "what looks right" rather than logical analysis. The correct answer is not always the one that seems most aesthetically pleasing—it is the one that follows the established rule. Always verify the rule before selecting an answer.
[/alert]

### Section 2 | The Two Major Question Types

| Type | Description | Approach |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pattern Completion** | A pattern with one missing segment | Identify the repeating or rotating pattern |
| **Figure Analogy** | Two figures show a transformation; apply it to complete the third | Identify the transformation rule |

## Chapter 2: Incomplete Patterns

### Section 1 | The Symmetry Rule

The most common rule in figure completion is **symmetry**. If the incomplete figure is part of a larger figure that is symmetrical, the missing part must make the figure symmetric.

**Types of Symmetry**:
- **Vertical Symmetry**: Left and right sides are mirror images.
- **Horizontal Symmetry**: Top and bottom are mirror images.
- **Diagonal Symmetry**: Reflected across a diagonal axis.
- **Rotational Symmetry**: The figure looks the same after rotation by a certain angle (usually 90°, 180°, or 270°).

**Example**: A square with one quadrant missing. The complete square should be symmetric. The missing quadrant must mirror the opposite quadrant.

### Section 2 | The Pattern Continuation Rule

Some figures are built from repeating elements (lines, curves, shapes) that follow a sequence. The missing part must continue the sequence.

**Example**: A 3×3 grid with shapes that rotate 90° clockwise each step. The missing cell must show the shape at the correct rotation.

**The Trap**: In grid patterns, the rule may apply horizontally, vertically, or diagonally. Always check all directions.

[alert type="success"]
**The Cross-Check Method**: Before selecting an answer, verify that it satisfies the rule in both the horizontal and vertical directions (and diagonally if applicable). A correct answer will pass all checks.
[/alert]

| Rule Type | Description | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Vertical Symmetry | Left mirrors right | A square with a missing left half |
| Horizontal Symmetry | Top mirrors bottom | A circle with missing bottom arc |
| Diagonal Symmetry | Mirrored across diagonal | A diamond with missing corner |
| Rotational Symmetry | Rotated by 90°, 180°, 270° | A flower with missing petal |
| Pattern Continuation | Sequence of shapes or rotations | Grid of arrows |
[/flashcards]

## Chapter 3: Missing Part of Figure (Analogy Approach)

### Section 1 | The Analogy Structure

Figure analogy completion presents a pair of figures (A and B) that show a transformation, and then a third figure (C) with a missing part (D). You must apply the same transformation from A to B to complete C.

**The Structure**:
- Figure A → Figure B (some transformation)
- Figure C → Figure D (same transformation)

**Example**:
- A: A square with a dot in the top-left corner.
- B: The same square with the dot in the bottom-right corner.
- The transformation is: the dot moves diagonally from top-left to bottom-right.
- C: A circle with a dot in the top-left corner.
- D: The circle with the dot in the bottom-right corner.

### Section 2 | Common Transformations

| Transformation | Description | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Rotation | Figure rotates by a fixed angle | 90° clockwise |
| Reflection | Figure is mirrored | Flipped horizontally |
| Translation | Figure moves to a new position | Shifted down and right |
| Scaling | Figure changes size | Enlarged or reduced |
| Pattern Addition | Elements are added or removed | A line is added |
| Pattern Replacement | One shape replaces another | Circle becomes square |

[alert type="warning"]
**The Complex Transformation**: Some figures combine multiple transformations. For example, a figure may rotate 90° and then change color. Apply each transformation sequentially to the third figure.
[/alert]

[flashcards title="Analogy Transformations"]
| Transformation | Figure A → Figure B | Figure C → Figure D |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Rotation | Triangle points up → Triangle points right | Triangle points left → Triangle points down |
| Reflection | Arrow points right → Arrow points left | Circle with dot right → Circle with dot left |
| Translation | Dot in center → Dot at bottom-left | Dot in center → Dot at bottom-left |
| Scaling | Small square → Large square | Small circle → Large circle |
[/flashcards]

[example]
**Question:** Figure A is a square with a diagonal line from top-left to bottom-right. Figure B is the same square with a diagonal line from top-right to bottom-left. Figure C is a circle with a horizontal line. What is Figure D?
**Solution:**
Step 1: Identify the transformation from A to B. The diagonal line flipped from one diagonal to the other. This is a reflection across the vertical axis? Actually, the diagonal from top-left to bottom-right flips to top-right to bottom-left—this is a reflection across the horizontal axis (or a 90° rotation? Let's check: A diagonal from TL to BR, when reflected horizontally, becomes TR to BL. Yes, that's a horizontal reflection).
Step 2: Apply the same transformation to Figure C. Figure C has a horizontal line. Reflecting horizontally across a horizontal axis leaves a horizontal line unchanged? Wait: Reflecting a horizontal line across a horizontal axis leaves it unchanged because it is symmetric about that axis. But the transformation from A to B was a reflection across the horizontal axis? Actually, A has a line from TL to BR. Reflect across vertical axis: TL → TR, BR → BL, so the line becomes TR to BL. That matches Figure B. So the transformation is vertical reflection.
Step 3: Apply vertical reflection to Figure C. A horizontal line reflected vertically remains a horizontal line. So Figure D is a circle with a horizontal line.
**Final Answer:** Circle with a horizontal line.
[/example]

## Chapter 4: Rotation and Reflection Based Completion

### Section 1 | Rotation Rules

When a figure rotates, every point moves around a center by a fixed angle. The most common angles are 90°, 180°, and 270°.

**The Rotation Checklist**:
1. **Identify the center of rotation** (usually the center of the figure).
2. **Determine the angle** from the known transformation.
3. **Apply the rotation** to the incomplete figure.
4. **Select the matching option**.

**Example**: A figure with three arrows arranged 120° apart. Rotate by 120° to complete the pattern. This is common in multiple-choice questions where the missing figure is one of the options.

### Section 2 | Reflection Rules

Reflection flips the figure across an axis. The axis can be vertical, horizontal, or diagonal.

**The Reflection Checklist**:
1. **Identify the axis of reflection**.
2. **Flip the figure** across that axis.
3. **Select the matching option**.

**The Trap**: Some figures are nearly symmetric, and the reflection might create a figure that looks similar but has subtle differences. Always check the orientation of lines and shapes, not just the overall silhouette.

[alert type="info"]
**The Combined Operation**: Some questions require both rotation and reflection. For example, a figure might be reflected across a vertical axis and then rotated 90° clockwise. Apply the operations in the order given.
[/alert]

[flashcards title="Rotation and Reflection Quick Reference"]
| Operation | Effect | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 90° CW Rotation | Every point moves 90° clockwise | Arrow up → Arrow right |
| 180° Rotation | Every point moves 180° | Arrow up → Arrow down |
| 270° CW Rotation | Same as 90° CCW | Arrow up → Arrow left |
| Vertical Reflection | Flipped left-right | Arrow right → Arrow left |
| Horizontal Reflection | Flipped top-bottom | Arrow up → Arrow down |
| Diagonal Reflection | Flipped across diagonal | Arrow up-right → Arrow up-left |
[/flashcards]

[example]
**Question:** A figure is a square with a smaller square in the top-right corner. The figure is rotated 90° clockwise. Which option shows the result?
**Solution:**
Step 1: Rotate the entire figure 90° clockwise about the center.
Step 2: The top-right corner moves to the bottom-right corner.
Step 3: The small square in the top-right corner moves to the bottom-right corner.
Step 4: The final figure has a small square in the bottom-right corner.
**Final Answer:** Square with a small square in the bottom-right corner.
[/example]

[quiz title="Figure Completion Mastery Quiz"]
Q: A figure has three petals pointing up, right, and down. The missing petal is at which position to complete the rotational symmetry?
- A) Left (Correct: For 90° rotational symmetry, the missing petal should point left.)
- B) Right
- C) Up
- D) Down

Q: A 3×3 grid shows arrows. The arrows in row 1 point up, up, up. Row 2 points right, right, right. Row 3 points down, down, ?. What is the missing arrow?
- A) Down (Correct: The pattern is consistent: each row has arrows pointing in the same direction, and the direction rotates 90° clockwise each row. Row 3 should point down.)
- B) Left
- C) Up
- D) Right

Q: Figure A shows a circle with a dot on the left. Figure B shows a circle with a dot on the right. Figure C shows a triangle with a dot on the left. What is Figure D?
- A) Triangle with dot on the right (Correct: The transformation is horizontal reflection. The dot moves from left to right.)
- B) Triangle with dot on top
- C) Triangle with dot on bottom
- D) Circle with dot on left

Q: A figure is rotated 180°. The original has a square in the top-left corner. Where is the square after rotation?
- A) Top-left
- B) Top-right
- C) Bottom-left
- D) Bottom-right (Correct: 180° rotation moves top-left to bottom-right.)

Q: A 4×4 pattern has a line pattern. The missing quadrant must complete the diagonal symmetry. Which option completes it?
- A) The mirror image of the opposite quadrant (Correct: Diagonal symmetry requires the missing quadrant to be the mirror image of the opposite quadrant.)
- B) The same as the adjacent quadrant
- C) The same as the opposite quadrant
- D) A random pattern
[/quiz]
