# Water Image

> The reflection of logic: decoding the vertical inversion that transforms objects as if viewed on the surface of a still lake. This is not about mirror images; it is about recognizing the top-bottom reversal that occurs in water reflection.

## Chapter 1: The Foundations of Water Image Logic

### Section 1 | What Is a Water Image?

A water image is the reflection of an object when viewed from below—as if the object is reflected in a horizontal plane (the surface of water). The effect is a **vertical inversion**: the top becomes bottom, and the bottom becomes top. Unlike a mirror image (lateral inversion), a water image reverses the vertical axis.

**The Core Principle**: In a water image, every point is reflected across a horizontal axis. The order of elements from top to bottom is reversed, and each character is vertically inverted. The left-right order remains the same.

**The Cognitive Shift**: When solving water image questions, flip the object upside down. Do not reverse left and right—only top and bottom. This is the key distinction from mirror images.

[alert type="danger"]
**The Mirror-Water Confusion**: Students often confuse water images with mirror images. Remember: mirror = left-right reversal (lateral inversion); water = top-bottom reversal (vertical inversion). If you reverse left and right in a water image question, you will get the wrong answer.
[/alert]

### Section 2 | The Reflection Axis

| Image Type | Axis | Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mirror Image | Vertical | Left ↔ Right |
| Water Image | Horizontal | Top ↔ Bottom |

In a water image, the object is reflected across a horizontal line (the water surface). The order of elements from top to bottom reverses, while elements stay in the same left-right position.

## Chapter 2: Alphabets in Water Image

### Section 1 | Capital Letters

In a water image, capital letters are vertically inverted. Some letters remain unchanged (or nearly so) because they are horizontally symmetric.

**Horizontally Symmetric Capital Letters (look the same in water image)**:
`B, C, D, E, H, I, K, O, X`

**Letters that change significantly**:
- `A` becomes an upside-down A (∀)
- `F` becomes an upside-down F (⅂)
- `G` becomes an upside-down G (⅁)
- `J` becomes an upside-down J (ſ)
- `L` becomes an upside-down L (⅂)
- `M` becomes an upside-down M (W? Actually M becomes W when inverted vertically? Yes! M → W)
- `N` becomes an upside-down N (И)
- `P` becomes an upside-down P (Ԁ)
- `Q` becomes an upside-down Q (ℚ? Usually looks like a mirrored Q)
- `R` becomes an upside-down R (ᴚ)
- `S` becomes an upside-down S (S is not horizontally symmetric; it becomes Ƨ)
- `T` becomes an upside-down T (⊥)
- `U` becomes an upside-down U (∩)
- `V` becomes an upside-down V (Λ)
- `W` becomes an upside-down W (M)
- `Y` becomes an upside-down Y (⅄)
- `Z` becomes an upside-down Z (Z is not symmetric; becomes Ƨ? Actually Z becomes Z in a 180° rotation, but in vertical inversion, Z becomes Ƨ)

[alert type="success"]
**The M-W Swap**: In a water image, `M` becomes `W` and `W` becomes `M`. This is one of the most frequently tested transformations.
[/alert]

**Small letters and their water images**:
- `a` → upside-down a (ɐ)
- `b` → upside-down b (q? Actually b becomes p? No, vertical inversion of b is q? Let's check: b has a stem on the left; upside down, the stem is on the right, giving q. So b ↔ q)

- `d` → upside-down d (p? d has a stem on the right; upside down, the stem is on the left, giving p. So d ↔ p)

- `f` → upside-down f (ɟ)
- `g` → upside-down g (ƃ)
- `h` → upside-down h (ɥ)

- `j` → upside-down j (ɾ)

- `n` → upside-down n (u? Actually n becomes u when inverted vertically. So n ↔ u)
- `o` → upside-down o (o) - unchanged
- `p` → upside-down p (d? p becomes d when inverted? Check: p has a stem on the left; upside down, the stem is on the right, giving d. So p ↔ d)
- `q` → upside-down q (b? q becomes b when inverted. So q ↔ b)

## Chapter 3: Numbers in Water Image

### Section 1 | Numeric Inversion

Not all numbers remain the same in a water image. Memorize these common transformations:

| Number | Water Image | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0 | 0 | Unchanged |
| 1 | 1 | Unchanged (in most fonts) |
| 2 | S-like (Ƨ) | Inverted 2 looks like S |
| 3 | Ɛ | Inverted 3 becomes like E |
| 4 | ꟼ | Inverted 4 looks like P |
| 5 | S-like (S) | Inverted 5 becomes like S |
| 6 | 9 | 6 and 9 swap in vertical inversion |
| 7 | ⅂ | Inverted 7 looks like L |
| 8 | 8 | Unchanged |
| 9 | 6 | 9 and 6 swap |

**The Trap**: `6` and `9` are often tested. In a water image, they swap. In a mirror image, they also swap (but for different reasons). Be careful to apply the correct transformation based on the question type.

### Section 2 | Multi-Digit Numbers

For multi-digit numbers, the order of digits **does not reverse** in a water image—only the vertical position reverses. The left-right order stays the same.

**Example**: Water image of `123`
- Digits: 1 (unchanged), 2 (becomes Ƨ), 3 (becomes Ɛ)
- Order stays 1, 2, 3, but each digit is inverted.
- So the water image is `1 Ƨ Ɛ` (from left to right, each digit flipped).

**Contrast with Mirror Image**: In a mirror image, `123` becomes `Ɛ Ƨ 1` (order reversed and each digit flipped).

[alert type="info"]
**The Order Rule**: In a water image, the order of characters stays the same (no left-right reversal). Only the top-bottom orientation of each character changes. This is the simplest way to distinguish water image from mirror image answers.
[/alert]

## Chapter 4: Basic Figures in Water Image

### Section 1 | Geometric Shapes

For abstract figures, the water image is created by reflecting the figure across a horizontal axis. The topmost point becomes the bottommost, and vice versa.

| Shape | Water Image | Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Triangle (pointing up) | Triangle (pointing down) | △ becomes ▽ |
| Triangle (pointing down) | Triangle (pointing up) | ▽ becomes △ |
| Square | Square | Unchanged |
| Rectangle | Rectangle | Unchanged |
| Circle | Circle | Unchanged |
| Arrow pointing up | Arrow pointing down | Direction reverses vertically |
| Arrow pointing right | Arrow pointing right | Direction stays the same! |

**The Trap**: Arrows. An arrow pointing right is horizontally symmetric? Actually, it is not vertically symmetric. When reflected in water, an arrow pointing right still points right—its horizontal direction is unchanged. Only the vertical orientation of the arrow's body is affected.

### Section 2 | The Combined Figure

Some questions present a combination of shapes (e.g., a triangle with a circle inside). The water image reflects the entire figure across the horizontal axis. The relative positions of the shapes are preserved (a circle inside a triangle remains inside), but the entire composite is inverted vertically.

[alert type="success"]
**The Horizontal Flip**: For any figure, imagine flipping it over a horizontal line passing through its center. The top becomes bottom, the bottom becomes top, but left and right stay on the same sides. That is the water image.
[/alert]

[example]
**Question:** What is the water image of the word `QUIZ`?
**Solution:**
Step 1: Order stays the same: Q, U, I, Z.
Step 2: Invert each letter:
- Q → upside-down Q (ℚ, looks like a mirrored Q with a stem)
- U → upside-down U (∩)
- I → unchanged
- Z → upside-down Z (Z is unchanged in water image? Actually Z becomes Ƨ in vertical inversion. Check: Z has diagonal lines; upside down, it becomes Ƨ.)
Step 3: Combine: `ℚ ∩ I Ƨ`
**Final Answer:** `ℚ ∩ I Ƨ`
[/example]

[example]
**Question:** What is the water image of the clock showing 3:15?
**Solution:**
Step 1: A clock in a water image is not the same as a mirror image. In a water image, the clock is flipped vertically. The hour hand (pointing right at 3) will still point right, but its vertical position will invert. The minute hand (pointing up at 15) will point down.
Step 2: This is complex and rarely tested. Most exams stick to mirror images for clocks. If tested, the water image of a clock would show the hands inverted vertically while maintaining horizontal direction.
Step 3: The actual time displayed in the water image would require knowing the positions of the hands after vertical inversion.
**Final Answer:** This is beyond the typical exam scope; focus on mirror clocks.
[/example]
