# History: Delhi Sultanate - Slave & Khalji Dynasties

> From the slave who became a sultan to the market reformer who defied the Mongols—the turbulent birth of Muslim rule in India.

## Chapter 1: The Slave Dynasty—The Foundational Era

### Section 1 | The Political Context

The Delhi Sultanate began with the *victory* of *Muhammad Ghori* over *Prithviraj Chauhan* at the *Second Battle of Tarain* (1192 CE). Ghori *returned* to his *capital* in *Ghazni*, leaving his *slave-general* Qutbuddin Aibak to *govern* his *Indian* conquests.

**The Nature of the Mamluk System:**

- *Mamluk* means "slave" in Arabic—but these were *not* enslaved laborers. They were *military slaves*—men of *Turkic* origin who were *bought*, *trained*, and *manumitted* to serve as *elite* warriors.
- The Mamluks were *loyal* to their *masters*—they were *taught* to *fight*, *administer*, and *command*.
- The *Slave Dynasty* (also called the *Mamluk Dynasty*) was the *first* Islamic dynasty of India—it *ruled* from =1206 to 1290 CE=.

[alert type="warning"]
**Exam Trap:**
The term *Mamluk* is *not* synonymous with *slave* in the *conventional* sense. The Mamluks were *elite* warriors—they were *not* enslaved laborers. The exam often tests this *distinction*—do not equate the Mamluks with *chattel slavery*.
[/alert]

---

### Section 2 | Qutbuddin Aibak (1206–1210 CE)—The First Sultan

Qutbuddin Aibak was the *first* Muslim ruler to *establish* an *independent* kingdom in India. He *ruled* from *Lahore* and *Delhi*.

**The Title: "Lakh Bakhsh" (Giver of Lakhs):**

- Qutbuddin earned the title *Lakh Bakhsh* because he was *generous*—he gave *vast* sums of *wealth* to *poets*, *scholars*, and *saints*.
- The *title* is *unique*—it *reflects* his *patronage* of *learning*.

**The Beginnings of the Qutub Minar:**

- Qutbuddin *started* the construction of the *Qutub Minar* in =1199 CE=—he *built* the *first* storey.
- The *minar* was *completed* by his *successor* Iltutmish and *later* repaired by *Firoz Shah Tughlaq*.

**The Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque:**

- Qutbuddin *built* the *Quwwat-ul-Islam* mosque at the *Qutub complex*—it was the *first* mosque in Delhi.
- It was *constructed* using *materials* from *Hindu* and *Jain* temples—a *common* practice of the early Sultanate period.

**The Death of Qutbuddin:**

- Qutbuddin *died* in =1210 CE=—he *fell* from his *horse* while *playing* polo (chaugan) in *Lahore*.
- He was *buried* in *Lahore*—his *tomb* is the *earliest* surviving Muslim tomb in India.

[alert type="success"]
**Important Fact:**
Qutbuddin Aibak is *often* called the "founder" of the Delhi Sultanate—but his *successor* Iltutmish *consolidated* the empire. The SSC tests this *distinction*: Qutbuddin was the *first* sultan, but Iltutmish was the *real* consolidator.
[/alert]

### Section 3 | Iltutmish (1210–1236 CE)—The Consolidator

Iltutmish was the *real* founder of the Delhi Sultanate—he *consolidated* the empire, *organized* the administration, and *resisted* the *Mongol* threat.

**The Ilbari Turks:**

- Iltutmish was a *Turk* of the *Ilbari* tribe—he was *bought* by Qutbuddin and *rose* to *prominence*.
- He *married* Qutbuddin's *daughter*—he *succeeded* him after a *brief* succession struggle.

**The Iqtadari System:**

- Iltutmish *introduced* the *Iqtadari* system—the *assignment* of *land* (iqtas) to *nobles* in *exchange* for *military* service.
- The *Iqtadars* (landholders) *collected* taxes from *their* territories—they *sent* the *revenue* to the *sultan*.

**Coinage: The Silver Tanka**

- Iltutmish *introduced* the *silver tanka*—the *first* silver coin of the Delhi Sultanate.
- The *tanka* became the *standard* currency of the Sultanate—it *remained* in use *until* the *Mughal* period.

[alert type="info"]
**Examiner's Focus:**
The *silver tanka* is *often* tested—it was *introduced* by Iltutmish. The *copper jital* was *also* introduced by him. The exam asks for the *coinage* system of the Sultanate—know the *tanka* (silver) and *jital* (copper).
[/alert]

**The Mongol Threat:**

- The *Mongols* (under *Genghis Khan*) had *conquered* *Central Asia*—they *threatened* the *north-western* frontier of India.
- Iltutmish *repelled* the Mongol *attacks*—he *protected* the *frontier* of the Sultanate.

**The Succession:**

- Iltutmish *chose* his *daughter* *Raziya* as his *successor*—he *overlooked* his *sons* because he *considered* them *incompetent*.
- His *decision* was *controversial*—but it *established* a *precedent* for a *woman* to *rule*.

[/flashcards]

### Section 4 | Raziya Sultan (1236–1240 CE)—The First Woman Ruler

Raziya Sultan was the *first* and *only* woman to *rule* the Delhi Sultanate. She *ascended* the throne in =1236 CE=—and *ruled* for *four* years.

**The Struggle for Power:**

- Raziya *faced* *opposition* from the *nobles*—they *resented* being *ruled* by a *woman*.
- She *wore* male *attire* and *led* her *army* in *battle*—she *defied* the *gender* conventions of her *time**.

**The Deposition:**

- Raziya was *deposed* in =1240 CE=—she was *killed* by *bandits* while *trying* to *recapture* the *throne*.
- Her *reign* was *short*—but it *remains* a *landmark* in *Indian* history.

[alert type="warning"]
**Exam Trap:**
Raziya Sultan is *not* the *only* woman ruler of India—but she is the *only* woman to *rule* the *Delhi Sultanate*. The exam often tests this *specificity*—do not *generalize* her as the "first woman ruler of India" (the *Rani of Jhansi* and *Chand Bibi* are *later*).
[/alert]

---

### Section 5 | Balban (1266–1287 CE)—The Persianized Sultan

Balban was the *greatest* sultan of the *Slave Dynasty*—he *restored* the *prestige* of the *crown* and *repelled* the *Mongol* threat.

**The "Blood and Iron" Policy:**

- Balban *adopted* the *Blood and Iron* policy—he *crushed* all *opposition* with *extreme* *severity*.
- He *appointed* *spies* in *every* *province*—he *executed* *rebels* and *conspirators* *mercilessly*.

**The Theory of Kingship:**

- Balban *believed* in the *divine* right of the *king*—he *claimed* to be the *shadow of God* (zil-e-illahi).
- He *adopted* Persian *ceremonial* and *court* *etiquette*—he *introduced* the *sajda* (prostration) before the *sultan*.

[alert type="info"]
**Examiner's Focus:**
Balban's *theory of kingship* is *often* tested—he was the *first* sultan to *claim* *divine* authority. The *Persian* influence on his *court* is *also* significant—he *encouraged* Persian *literature* and *culture*.
[/alert]

**The Mongol Threat (Continued):**

- Balban *repelled* the *Mongol* attacks on the *north-western* frontier—he *strengthened* the *border* forts.
- He *built* the *forts* of *Kailugat* and *Ghaznin* to *defend* the *frontier*.

**The Administration:**

- Balban *strengthened* the *Iqtadari* system—he *appointed* *loyal* *nobles* to *administrative* positions.
- He *protected* the *peasants* from *excessive* *taxation*—he *maintained* the *army* and *patronized* *poets*.

[flashcards title="Balban Essentials"]

|------|--------|
| Reign | 1266–1287 CE |
| Policy | Blood and Iron (severe punishment) |
| Theory of Kingship | Shadow of God (zil-e-illahi) |
| Persian influence | Court etiquette, sajda |
| Mongol threat | Repelled attacks |
| End | Died 1287; succession struggle |
[/flashcards]

## Chapter 2: The Khalji Dynasty—The Imperial Phase

### Section 1 | The Rise of the Khaljis

The *Khaljis* were *Turko-Afghans*—they *overthrew* the *Slave Dynasty* in =1290 CE=. The *Khalji* period (1290–1320 CE) was *marked* by *imperial* expansion and *centralized* administration.

**Jalaluddin Khalji (1290–1296 CE)—The Liberal Sultan:**

- Jalaluddin was the *first* Khalji sultan—he *assumed* the throne at *over* 70 years of age.
- He was *liberal* and *generous*—he *abolished* *taxes* on *grain* and *tolerated* *non-Muslims*.
- He was *assassinated* by his *nephew* *Alauddin* in =1296 CE=.

---

### Section 2 | Alauddin Khalji (1296–1316 CE)—The Conqueror

Alauddin Khalji was the *greatest* sultan of the *Khalji* dynasty—he *expanded* the empire, *repelled* the *Mongols*, and *introduced* *market* *controls*.

**The Conquests:**

| Region | Year | Significance |
|--------|------|--------------|
| Gujarat | 1299 CE | Wealthy trade region; looted |
| Malwa | 1305 CE | Conquered Paramara kingdom |
| Rajasthan | 1303 CE | Ranthambore, Chittor conquered |
| Deccan | 1307–1313 CE | Malik Kafur led campaigns; Warangal, Dwarasamudra, Madurai |

**The Deccan Campaigns (Malik Kafur):**

- Alauddin's *general* Malik Kafur *led* the *southern* campaigns.
- He *conquered* the *Yadavas* of *Devagiri*, the *Kakatiyas* of *Warangal*, and the *Hoysalas* of *Dwarasamudra*.
- He *marched* to *Madurai*—he *subjugated* the *Pandyas*.
- The *southern* campaigns *brought* *vast* *wealth* to the *Sultanate*.

[alert type="success"]
**Important Fact:**
Alauddin Khalji's *Deccan* campaigns were *motivated* by *wealth*—he *looted* the *temples* and *treasuries* of the *southern* kings. The *wealth* was *used* to *maintain* a *large* army and *fund* the *market* controls.
[/alert]

**The Mongol Threat:**

- Alauddin Khalji *faced* the *Mongol* threat *repeatedly*—the Mongols *attacked* the *north-western* frontier in =1297–1305 CE=.
- He *built* *forts* and *strengthened* the *garrisons*—he *repelled* the *Mongols* with *heavy* *casualties*.
- The *Mongol* attacks *stopped* after =1305 CE=—the *frontier* was *secured*.

[flashcards title="Alauddin Khalji Essentials"]
| Fact | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Reign | 1296–1316 CE |
| Conquests | Gujarat (1299), Malwa (1305), Deccan (1307–1313) |
| General | Malik Kafur (led Deccan campaigns) |
| Mongol threat | Repelled (1297–1305) |
| Market controls | Diwan-i-Riyasat; price controls |
| End | Died 1316; succession crisis |
[/flashcards]

## Chapter 3: Alauddin's Market Control—The Price Revolution

### Section 1 | The Four Markets

Alauddin Khalji *introduced* a *system* of *price controls*—the *most* *famous* economic reform of the *Sultanate*.

**The Four Markets (Mandis):**

| Market | Goods |
|--------|-------|
| Food grains | Wheat, barley, rice, pulses |
| Cloth | Textiles, silk, cotton |
| Slaves | Slave market |
| Horses | Horses for the army |

**The Diwan-i-Riyasat:**

- Alauddin *appointed* the *Diwan-i-Riyasat* (the *officer* of the *markets*) to *regulate* the *prices*.
- The *officer* was *responsible* for *enforcing* the *prices* and *punishing* *hoarders*.

**State Stockpiling:**

- Alauddin *ordered* the *state* to *stockpile* *grain* in *government* *granaries*.
- The *stockpiles* were *used* to *release* grain *during* *shortages*—they *stabilized* the *prices*.

[alert type="warning"]
**Exam Trap:**
The *Diwan-i-Riyasat* is *not* the *same* as the *Diwan-i-Amir Kohi* (which was the *agricultural* department). The *Diwan-i-Riyasat* *regulated* the *market*; the *Diwan-i-Amir Kohi* *managed* the *land* revenue. The exam tests this *distinction*.
[/alert]

**Strict Punishments:**

- Alauddin *ordered* *severe* *punishments* for *hoarders* and *profiteers*—they were *imprisoned*, *fined*, and *publicly* *humiliated*.
- The *strict* enforcement *ensured* that *prices* *remained* *stable*.

**The Rationale:**

- Alauddin *needed* a *large* army to *repel* the *Mongols*.
- A *large* army *required* *low* *prices*—the *soldiers* needed to be *paid* a *low* *wage*.
- The *price controls* *enabled* Alauddin to *maintain* a *standing* army at *low* *cost*.

[flashcards title="Alauddin's Market Reforms"]
| Reform | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Four markets | Grain, cloth, slaves, horses |
| Diwan-i-Riyasat | Officer of the markets |
| Stockpiling | State granaries for grain |
| Punishments | Severe for hoarders |
| Rationale | Maintain large army at low cost |
[/flashcards]

## Chapter 4: Alauddin's Administrative Reforms

### Section 1 | Land Revenue System

Alauddin *introduced* a *new* land revenue system—the *Kharaj* tax was *raised* to =50%= of the *produce*.

**The Assessment:**

- The land was *measured* and *assessed*—the *revenue* was *fixed* in *cash* (not *kind*).
- The *peasants* had to *pay* the *tax* in *silver* *tankas*—they *had* to *sell* their *grain* in the *market*.

**Khalisa Lands:**

- Alauddin *declared* a *large* portion of *land* as *Khalisa* (state land).
- The *revenue* from *Khalisa* lands *went* *directly* to the *state* treasury.
- The *nobles* were *not* given *land grants* (iqtas) on *Khalisa* lands—they *became* *salaried* *officials*.

---

### Section 2 | The Permanent Army

Alauddin *maintained* a *large* standing army—he *paid* his *soldiers* *cash* *salaries* (not *land* grants).

**The Horse Market:**

- Alauddin *controlled* the *horse* market—he *fixed* the *price* of *horses*.
- The *soldiers* were *required* to *maintain* *their* own *horses*—they were *inspected* *regularly*.

**The Introspection System:**

- Alauddin *used* *spies* to *monitor* the *nobles* and *officials*.
- The *spies* *reported* *directly* to the *sultan*—they *ensured* that the *nobles* were *loyal* and *obedient*.

[alert type="info"]
**Examiner's Focus:**
Alauddin's *introspection* system is *often* compared to *Kautilya's* spy network—both were *centralized* *surveillance* systems. The exam sometimes tests this *parallel*.
[/alert]

[flashcards title="Alauddin's Administrative Reforms"]
| Reform | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Kharaj | 50% of produce |
| Khalisa lands | State lands (revenue to treasury) |
| Permanent army | Cash salaries; horse market controlled |
| Introspection system | Spies to monitor nobles |
[/flashcards]

## Chapter 5: Art and Architecture of the Khalji Period

### Section 1 | The Qutub Minar Complex

The *Qutub Minar* was *completed* by *Iltutmish*—but it was *damaged* by *lightning* and *repaired* by *Alauddin Khalji*.

**The Alai Darwaza (1311 CE):**

- Alauddin *built* the *Alai Darwaza*—a *gateway* to the *Quwwat-ul-Islam* mosque.
- It is the *first* structure in India to *use* a *true arch* and *true dome*—the *earliest* example of *Islamic* architecture in India.

**The Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque:**

- The *mosque* was *built* by *Qutbuddin* Aibak—it *used* *materials* from *Hindu* and *Jain* temples.
- Alauddin *added* the *Alai Darwaza* and *extended* the *mosque*.

**The Siri Fort:**

- Alauddin *built* the *Siri Fort* as a *new* capital—he *used* it to *house* his *army*.
- The *fort* was *constructed* to *defend* against the *Mongol* attacks.

---

### Section 2 | The Architectural Innovations

**The True Arch and Dome:**

- The *Alai Darwaza* *introduced* the *true arch* and *true dome* to India.
- The *arch* is *made* of *radiating* stones—it *transfers* the *weight* to the *sides*.
- The *dome* is *built* of *concentric* rings—it *covers* the *central* space.

[alert type="warning"]
**Exam Trap:**
The *true arch* is *not* the *same* as the *corbelled arch*. The *corbelled arch* (used in *Hindu* temples) *projects* stones *inward*—it *does not* require a *keystone*. The *true arch* (introduced by *Muslims*) *uses* a *keystone*—it is a *stronger* and *more stable* structure. The exam tests this *engineering* distinction.
[/alert]

[flashcards title="Khalji Architecture"]
| Monument | King | Innovation |
|----------|------|------------|
| Qutub Minar | Completed by Iltutmish | Tallest minaret |
| Alai Darwaza | Alauddin Khalji | True arch and true dome |
| Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque | Qutbuddin Aibak | Spolia (Hindu/Jain materials) |
| Siri Fort | Alauddin Khalji | New capital for army |
[/flashcards]

## Chapter 6: Literature of the Sultanate Period

### Section 1 | Amir Khusrau—The Nightingale of India

Amir Khusrau (1253–1325 CE) was the *greatest* poet of the *Sultanate* period—he *served* as the *court poet* of *Alauddin Khalji*.

**The Inventions:**

- Amir Khusrau is *credited* with *inventing* the *sitar* and *tabla*—he *blended* *Persian* and *Indian* musical traditions.
- He *also* *invented* the *qawwali*—the *devotional* music of the *Sufis*.

**The Works:**

| Work | Genre | Significance |
|------|-------|--------------|
| Tughlaq Namah | Historical poem | Description of Tughlaq period |
| Khazain-ul-Futuh | Account of Alauddin's conquests | Historical source |
| Nuh Sipihr | Poetic work | Praise of India |
| Ashiqa | Romance | Love story of Khizr Khan and Deval Devi |

[alert type="success"]
**Important Fact:**
Amir Khusrau is *called* the "Nightingale of India" (Tuti-e-Hind). He *wrote* in *Persian*, *Hindustani*, and *Braj Bhasha*—he was a *linguistic* genius. The exam tests his *association* with *Alauddin Khalji* and his *inventions* (sitar, tabla, qawwali).
[/alert]

---

### Section 2 | Ziauddin Barani—The Historian

Ziauddin Barani was a *historian* of the *Sultanate* period—he *wrote* the *Tarikh-i-Firozshahi*.

**The Tarikh-i-Firozshahi:**

- The *Tarikh-i-Firozshahi* is a *history* of the *Delhi Sultanate* from *Balban* to *Firoz Shah Tughlaq*.
- It is a *primary* source for the *Khalji* and *Tughlaq* periods.
- Barani was *critical* of the *Sultans*—he *condemned* *Alauddin's* *market* controls and *religious* policies.

[flashcards title="Sultanate Literature"]
| Author | Work | Significance |
|--------|------|--------------|
| Amir Khusrau | Khazain-ul-Futuh | Court poet of Alauddin |
| Amir Khusrau | Inventions | Sitar, tabla, qawwali |
| Ziauddin Barani | Tarikh-i-Firozshahi | History of the Sultanate |
[/flashcards]

## Chapter 7: Synthesis—The Legacy of the Slave and Khalji Dynasties

### Section 1 | The Conceptual Takeaway

1. **Slave Dynasty**: The *foundation* of Muslim rule in India—the *military* slaves who *became* kings.

2. **Khalji Dynasty**: The *imperial* phase—the *conquest* of the *Deccan* and the *repulsion* of the *Mongols*.

3. **Market Reforms**: Alauddin's *price controls*—the *most* *ambitious* economic reform of the *Sultanate*.

4. **Architecture**: The *true arch* and *dome*—the *Islamic* influence on *Indian* building traditions.

5. **Literature**: Amir Khusrau—the *synthesis* of *Persian* and *Indian* cultures.

## Chapter 8: The Final Memorization Grid

### Section 1 | Chronological Summary

| Dynasty | Period | Greatest King | Key Achievement |
|---------|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| Slave (Mamluk) | 1206–1290 CE | Iltutmish (1210–1236) | Consolidated Sultanate; Iqtadari; Silver Tanka |
| Slave (Mamluk) | 1206–1290 CE | Balban (1266–1287) | Blood and Iron policy; Theory of Kingship |
| Khalji | 1290–1320 CE | Alauddin Khalji (1296–1316) | Deccan conquests; Market controls; Alai Darwaza |

### Section 2 | The Examination Mindset

1. **Identify the "Which":** Is the question asking about a *king*, a *reform*, a *monument*, or a *text*? Your mental checklist: *Person*, *Policy*, *Place*, *Text*.

2. **Identify the "When":** The *Slave Dynasty* is *earlier* (1206–1290); the *Khalji Dynasty* is *later* (1290–1320). The *chronological* sequence is: Qutbuddin → Iltutmish → Raziya → Balban → Jalaluddin → Alauddin.

3. **Identify the "What":** The *Iqtadari* system (land grants), the *market* controls (Diwan-i-Riyasat), the *true arch* (Alai Darwaza), the *silver tanka* (Iltutmish).

4. **Avoid the "Mongol Myth":** The Mongols *did not* conquer India—the Delhi Sultans *repelled* them. The exam tests the *defense* against the Mongols—*not* their conquest.

[alert type="success"]
**Final Exam Mantra:**
The Slave and Khalji dynasties *established* the *institutions* of the Delhi Sultanate—the *Iqtadari* system, the *coinage*, the *army*, and the *market* controls. The *architecture* and *literature* of this period *synthesized* *Persian* and *Indian* traditions. The *examination* of this period is *not* about *memorizing* all the kings—it is about *understanding* the *institutions* that *defined* the Sultanate. When you *understand* these *institutions*, you will answer *any* question correctly.
[/alert]
