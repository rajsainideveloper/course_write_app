## Chapter 25: Phrasal Verbs—Get, Give, Go, Hold, Keep

### Section 25.1 | The Cognitive Architecture of Extended Phrasal Verb Families

The phrasal verbs covered in this chapter represent the most versatile and frequently used combinations in English. The verbs *get*, *give*, *go*, *hold*, and *keep* each combine with multiple particles to produce meanings ranging from the literal to the highly idiomatic.

**The Cognitive Rule:** =Each verb family forms a semantic network. The core meaning of the verb is modified by the particle's directional or aspectual meaning. Understanding this network allows you to deduce unfamiliar phrasal verbs rather than memorizing each one in isolation.=

The examination pattern for these families is consistent: SSC and Bank PO papers test 3-5 questions from this group per examination. The *get* family alone accounts for nearly 15% of all phrasal verb questions.

### Section 25.2 | The Get Family

*Get* is the most versatile verb in English. Combined with particles, it produces meanings related to acquisition, movement, communication, and emotional states.

#### The Particle Logic for Get

| Particle | Core Logic | Meaning Cluster |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Along | Progress, relationship | Harmony, cooperation |
| Away | Movement from, escape | Escape, departure, removal |
| By | Passing, managing | Survival, adequate performance |
| Over | Overcoming, recovery | Recovery, acceptance, completion |
| Through | Completion, endurance | Finishing, surviving, passing |
| Up | Rising, preparing | Rising, preparing, organizing |
| Out | Exit, information | Leaving, extracting, publishing |

#### Get Along

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To have a good relationship with someone |
| Meaning 2 | To manage or cope with a situation |
| Meaning 3 | To progress or advance |
| Transitivity | Intransitive |
| Particle Logic | "Along" = forward movement, cooperation |

**Usage Examples:**
- "She gets along well with her colleagues." (Good relationship)
- "He is getting along fine without help." (Manage)
- "How are you getting along with the project?" (Progress)

[alert type="warning"]
**Key Distinction:** "Get along" = *have a good relationship* or *manage*. "Get on" can also mean "have a good relationship" (especially in British English). Examiners sometimes present both as options—"get along" is the more common American usage. "Get on with" = continue or have a relationship. The preposition following the phrasal verb determines meaning.
[/alert]

#### Get Away

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To escape (from a situation, place) |
| Meaning 2 | To take a vacation/break |
| Meaning 3 | To avoid punishment |
| Transitivity | Intransitive |
| Particle Logic | "Away" = movement from, escape |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The thief got away." (Escape)
- "We need to get away for the weekend." (Vacation)
- "He got away with cheating." (Avoid punishment—note the additional particle "with")

#### Get By

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To manage or survive (with limited resources) |
| Meaning 2 | To pass (without being noticed) |
| Meaning 3 | To move past (physically) |
| Transitivity | Intransitive |
| Particle Logic | "By" = passing, adequate navigation |

**Usage Examples:**
- "They get by on a small income." (Manage)
- "She got by without speaking English." (Manage)
- "I got by the security guard unnoticed." (Pass without being noticed)

#### Get Over

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To recover from (illness, emotional distress) |
| Meaning 2 | To overcome (an obstacle, problem) |
| Meaning 3 | To accept or cope with (bad news) |
| Transitivity | Transitive inseparable |
| Particle Logic | "Over" = overcoming, passing beyond |

**Usage Examples:**
- "It took her months to get over the flu." (Recover from illness)
- "He never got over his ex-girlfriend." (Recover emotionally)
- "We must get over this problem." (Overcome)

#### Get Through

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To complete or finish (a task, period of time) |
| Meaning 2 | To survive (a difficult period) |
| Meaning 3 | To successfully communicate or make contact |
| Meaning 4 | To pass (an exam, selection) |
| Transitivity | Transitive inseparable |
| Particle Logic | "Through" = completion, survival, successful passage |

**Usage Examples:**
- "She got through all the work in one day." (Complete)
- "We got through the crisis together." (Survive)
- "I tried to call, but I couldn't get through." (Make contact)
- "He got through the interview successfully." (Pass)

#### Get Up

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To rise from bed or a seat |
| Meaning 2 | To prepare or organize (an event, group) |
| Meaning 3 | To increase (speed, momentum) |
| Transitivity | Intransitive; Transitive separable (meaning 2, 3) |
| Particle Logic | "Up" = rising, increasing, preparing |

**Usage Examples:**
- "She gets up at 6 am every day." (Rise from bed)
- "The class got up when the teacher entered." (Stand)
- "The students got up a charity event." (Organize)
- "The car got up speed on the highway." (Increase)

#### Get Out

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To leave or exit |
| Meaning 2 | To extract (information, truth) |
| Meaning 3 | To publish or make public |
| Meaning 4 | To remove (a stain, mark) |
| Transitivity | Intransitive; Transitive separable (meanings 2, 3, 4) |
| Particle Logic | "Out" = exit, extraction, public disclosure |

**Usage Examples:**
- "Get out of the building immediately." (Exit)
- "The police got the truth out of him." (Extract information)
- "The magazine gets out monthly." (Publish)
- "I can't get this stain out." (Remove)

### Section 25.3 | The Give Family

*Give* combined with particles produces meanings related to transfer, concession, and emission.

#### The Particle Logic for Give

| Particle | Core Logic | Meaning Cluster |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Away | Donation, loss | Giving freely, losing |
| Back | Return | Returning, reciprocating |
| In | Surrender, concession | Yielding, ceasing resistance |
| Off | Emission, release | Emitting (light, heat, smell) |
| Out | Distribution, exhaustion | Distributing, failing |
| Up | Surrender, cessation | Stopping, sacrificing, surrendering |
| Way | Collapse, yielding | Giving ground, breaking |

#### Give Away

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To give something for free (as a gift) |
| Meaning 2 | To reveal a secret unintentionally |
| Meaning 3 | To betray or expose someone |
| Transitivity | Transitive separable |
| Particle Logic | "Away" = donation, loss of control |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The company gave away free samples." (Gift)
- "His expression gave away his true feelings." (Reveal)
- "She gave away the surprise party." (Reveal secret)

#### Give Back

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To return something to its owner |
| Meaning 2 | To restore (something taken away) |
| Meaning 3 | To reciprocate (kindness, favor) |
| Transitivity | Transitive separable |
| Particle Logic | "Back" = return, reciprocity |

**Usage Examples:**
- "Please give back my book." (Return)
- "The committee gave back the decision." (Restore)
- "She wanted to give back to her community." (Reciprocate)

#### Give In

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To surrender (to pressure, demands) |
| Meaning 2 | To cease resisting |
| Meaning 3 | To yield (in an argument) |
| Transitivity | Intransitive |
| Particle Logic | "In" = concession, surrender |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The government gave in to the protesters' demands." (Surrender)
- "I gave in and ate the chocolate." (Cease resistance)

[alert type="danger"]
**Critical Distinction:** "Give in" = *surrender or yield*. "Give up" = *stop trying, quit, or surrender*. These are often confused. "Give in" implies yielding to an external force or pressure. "Give up" implies ceasing effort on one's own accord or surrendering something. "He gave in to his parents' demands" (yielded). "He gave up his seat" (sacrificed). "He gave up trying" (quit). Examiners test this distinction repeatedly.
[/alert]

#### Give Off

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To emit (light, heat, smell, sound) |
| Meaning 2 | To release (energy, particles) |
| Transitivity | Transitive separable |
| Particle Logic | "Off" = emission, release outward |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The flowers give off a pleasant fragrance." (Emit smell)
- "The heater gives off warm air." (Emit heat)

#### Give Out

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To distribute (to many people) |
| Meaning 2 | To announce or make public |
| Meaning 3 | To become exhausted or fail |
| Transitivity | Transitive separable (meaning 1, 2); Intransitive (meaning 3) |
| Particle Logic | "Out" = distribution, depletion |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The teacher gave out the test papers." (Distribute)
- "The government gave out the official statement." (Announce)
- "His energy gave out after the marathon." (Become exhausted)

#### Give Up

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To cease trying or making effort |
| Meaning 2 | To surrender or yield |
| Meaning 3 | To sacrifice or relinquish something |
| Meaning 4 | To stop a habit |
| Transitivity | Intransitive (meaning 1, 2); Transitive separable (meaning 3, 4) |
| Particle Logic | "Up" = cessation, surrender |

**Usage Examples:**
- "Don't give up; keep trying." (Cease effort)
- "The criminal gave himself up to the police." (Surrender)
- "She gave up her career to raise children." (Sacrifice)
- "He gave up smoking." (Stop habit)

#### Give Way

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To collapse or break (structure) |
| Meaning 2 | To yield to (pressure, emotion) |
| Meaning 3 | To allow someone to pass or proceed |
| Transitivity | Intransitive |
| Particle Logic | "Way" = yielding, collapse |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The floor gave way under the weight." (Collapse)
- "She gave way to tears." (Yield to emotion)
- "Please give way to the ambulance." (Allow passage)

### Section 25.4 | The Go Family

*Go* combined with particles produces meanings related to movement, occurrence, and continuation.

#### The Particle Logic for Go

| Particle | Core Logic | Meaning Cluster |
|:---|:---|:---|
| After | Pursuit | Chase, seek |
| Ahead | Continuation | Continue, proceed, start |

| Back | Return | Return, reverse |
| Down | Decrease, descent | Decrease, fall, happen |
| Off | Depreciation, explosion | Explode, rot, happen |
| On | Continuation | Continue, proceed |
| Out | Extinction, departure | Extinguish, depart, become unfashionable |

#### Go After

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To pursue or chase (physically) |
| Meaning 2 | To seek or strive for (objectively, goal) |
| Transitivity | Transitive inseparable |
| Particle Logic | "After" = pursuit, following |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The police went after the escaped prisoner." (Chase)
- "He is going after the promotion." (Seek)

#### Go Ahead

| Aspect | Detail |

**Usage Examples:**

#### Go Along

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|

### Section 25.5 | The Hold Family

*Hold* combined with particles produces meanings related to restraint, continuation, and endurance.

#### The Particle Logic for Hold

| Particle | Core Logic | Meaning Cluster |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Back | Restraint | Withhold, delay, restrain |
| On | Continuation | Persevere, maintain |
| Out | Endurance | Last, persist, continue |
| Over | Delay, postponement | Postpone, extend |
| Up | Support, delay | Support, delay, rob |

#### Hold Back

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To restrain or prevent (physically) |
| Meaning 2 | To delay (progress, information) |
| Meaning 3 | To control (emotions, tears) |
| Meaning 4 | To keep (information) secret |
| Transitivity | Transitive separable |
| Particle Logic | "Back" = restraint, prevention, delay |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The police held back the crowd." (Restrain physically)
- "She held back her tears." (Control emotion)

The context is essential: "Hold back the tears" (restrain), "Hold on the line" (wait), "Hold up the traffic" (delay), "Hold up the structure" (support).
[/alert]

**The "Get Over" Priority:** Among all phrasal verbs across all families, "get over" (recover/overcome) is the single most frequently tested item. It appears in nearly every SSC and Bank PO English section. If you learn only one phrasal verb from this chapter, learn "get over."

- The Top 5: *Get over*, *Get through*, *Go on*, *Give up*, *Keep up*—these five appear in 70% of all phrasal verb questions. Prioritize them.

### Section 25.8 | Solved Examples

[example]
**Question:** It took her months to _____ her father's death.
- A) get over
- B) get through
- C) get along
- D) get by

**Solution:** The correct answer is **A) get over**. The sentence indicates recovery from emotional distress. "Get over" means to recover from illness or emotional pain. "Get through" (B) means to survive a difficult period or complete something. "Get along" (C) means to have a good relationship or manage. "Get by" (D) means to manage or survive. The context of "death" specifically requires the emotional recovery meaning of "get over."
[/example]

[example]
**Question:** The management finally _____ to the workers' demands after a long strike.
- A) gave up
- B) gave in
- C) gave away
- D) gave out

**Solution:** The correct answer is **B) gave in**. The sentence indicates surrender to external pressure (workers' demands). "Give in" means to yield or surrender to pressure. ### Section 25.10 | The Final Synthesis: Integrated Phrasal Verb Mastery

The mastery of phrasal verbs requires integration across families. The examination will not test them in isolation—a sentence may require knowledge from multiple families simultaneously.

**The Integration Framework:**

1. **The Semantic Field Approach:** Organize phrasal verbs by meaning rather than verb family.
   - *Continuation:* Carry on, Go on, Keep on, Hold on
   - *Surrender:* Give in, Give up, Give way
   - *Recovery:* Get over, Get through, Pull through
   - *Restraint:* Hold back, Keep back, Hold up (delay)

2. **The Context Sensitivity Principle:** The same phrasal verb can have multiple meanings based on context.
   - *Go off:* explode, ring, go bad, depart
   - *Get over:* recover from illness, recover emotionally, overcome
   - *Give out:* distribute, become exhausted, announce

3. **The Particle Consistency Principle:** A particle consistently modifies the verb in predictable ways across families.
   - `Up` = completion, increase, preparation: *get up, give up, keep up, hold up*
   - `Over` = overcoming, covering: *get over, go over, hold over*
   - `Back` = return, restraint: *give back, go back, hold back, keep back*

[alert type="success"]
**The Examination Edge:** Phrasal verbs constitute one of the most predictable sections of the English paper. The examination tests the same core set repeatedly—the Get, Give, Go, Hold, and Keep families in this chapter, and the Break, Call, Carry, Come, Cut, and Fall families from the previous chapter. A candidate who has systematically internalized these families has secured 5-6 questions before entering the examination room. This is preparation that directly translates to marks—no interpretation required, no ambiguity allowed. Master these families, and you master the section.
[/alert]
