# Phrasal Verbs (Part 1)

> The particle changes everything—a verb and a preposition together create meanings that neither word could achieve alone, forming the backbone of fluent English expression.

## Chapter 24: Phrasal Verbs—The Foundation

### Section 24.1 | The Architecture of Phrasal Verbs

A phrasal verb is a combination of a *verb* and one or more *particles* (prepositions or adverbs) that together function as a single semantic unit. The meaning of the phrasal verb is often non-compositional—it cannot be deduced from the meanings of its constituent parts.

**The Cognitive Rule:** =A phrasal verb is a single lexical item with a single meaning. Your brain must process "break down" as a single unit, not as "break" + "down." This chunking process is the key to fluency and examination success.=

Examiners test phrasal verbs in three primary patterns:
1. **Meaning Identification:** A phrasal verb is given; select the correct meaning.
2. **Contextual Fill:** A sentence has a blank; select the correct phrasal verb to complete it.
3. **Particle Selection:** The verb is fixed; select the correct particle (preposition/adverb) to complete the phrasal verb.

The challenge lies in the sheer number of phrasal verbs and the fact that the same verb with different particles can have entirely different meanings. *Look up*, *look after*, *look down on*, *look forward to*, *look into*—each is a distinct lexical item with a distinct meaning.

#### The Structural Types

| Type | Structure | Example | Note |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Intransitive | Verb + Particle | Break down (stop functioning) | No object required |
| Transitive Separable | Verb + Particle + Object | Pick up the book / Pick the book up | Object can go between verb and particle |
| Transitive Inseparable | Verb + Particle + Object | Look after the child | Object must follow the particle |
| Three-Part | Verb + Particle + Particle + Object | Put up with the noise | Two particles; inseparable |

**The Separation Rule:**
- =If the phrasal verb is *transitive and separable*, the object can go *before* or *after* the particle: "Turn on the light" OR "Turn the light on."=
- =If the object is a *pronoun*, it *must* go between the verb and the particle: "Turn it on" (NOT "Turn on it").=
- =If the phrasal verb is *intransitive*, no object follows: "The car broke down."=

[alert type="danger"]
**Critical Trap:** The separable/inseparable distinction is a frequent source of errors. "Call on" (visit) is inseparable: "Call on the doctor" (NOT "Call the doctor on"). "Call up" (telephone) is separable: "Call up the manager" OR "Call the manager up" AND "Call him up" (NOT "Call up him"). Examiners specifically test the pronoun placement rule.
[/alert]

### Section 24.2 | The Particle Logic

Understanding the core meanings of particles provides a systematic approach to phrasal verbs. While idiomatic meanings often deviate from literal patterns, the particle provides a reliable cognitive anchor.

**The Particle Architecture:**

| Particle | Core Meaning | Common Extension | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Up | Movement upward; completion; intensification | Increase; finish; appear | Break up, look up, give up |
| Down | Movement downward; reduction | Decrease; write; record | Break down, cut down, sit down |
| Off | Separation; departure | Disconnect; cancel; begin | Break off, cut off, take off |
| On | Continuation; connection | Continue; depend; activate | Carry on, put on, turn on |
| Out | Movement outward; removal | Exclude; discover; extinguish | Break out, find out, cut out |
| In | Movement inward; inclusion | Enter; submit; participate | Call in, give in, take in |
| Over | Movement across; repetition | Complete; review; control | Come over, go over, take over |
| Through | Movement from one side to another | Complete; penetrate; persist | Cut through, get through, go through |
| With | Accompaniment; opposition | Tolerate; contend; associate | Put up with, deal with, go with |
| Into | Movement to interior; investigation | Explore; crash; become | Break into, look into, run into |
| For | Purpose; objective; seeking | Seek; represent; support | Call for, look for, stand for |
| Away | Movement from; continuous action | Remove; continue | Carry away, give away, run away |

[alert type="info"]
**Memory Strategy:** When you encounter an unfamiliar phrasal verb, ask yourself: "What is the core meaning of the particle?" This provides a *hypothesis* about the meaning. Then confirm from context. This strategy is particularly useful in reading comprehension passages where unfamiliar phrasal verbs appear.
[/alert]

### Section 24.3 | The Break Family

The verb *break* with various particles produces some of the most frequently tested phrasal verbs in competitive examinations.

#### Break Down

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To stop functioning (machinery, vehicles) |
| Meaning 2 | To collapse emotionally (person) |
| Meaning 3 | To analyze into parts (data, argument) |
| Transitivity | Intransitive (meanings 1, 2); Transitive (meaning 3) |
| Particle Logic | "Down" = deterioration, cessation of function |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The car broke down on the highway." (Machinery failure)
- "After the tragedy, he broke down and wept." (Emotional collapse)
- "We need to break down the budget into categories." (Analyze)

[example]
**Question:** The committee decided to _____ the complex proposal into smaller components for better understanding.
- A) break up
- B) break down
- C) break out
- D) break off

**Solution:** The correct answer is **B) break down**. The sentence requires a phrasal verb meaning "to analyze into parts." "Break up" (A) means to separate or disperse; "break out" (C) means to escape or begin suddenly; "break off" (D) means to end abruptly.
[/example]

#### Break Into

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To enter illegally/forcibly |
| Meaning 2 | To interrupt (a conversation, activity) |
| Meaning 3 | To begin a new activity abruptly |
| Transitivity | Transitive inseparable |
| Particle Logic | "Into" = movement to interior |

**Usage Examples:**
- "Thieves broke into the warehouse at midnight." (Illegal entry)
- "She broke into the conversation to correct him." (Interrupt)
- "He broke into a run when he saw the bus." (Abrupt start)

#### Break Out

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To escape from confinement |
| Meaning 2 | To begin suddenly (war, disease, fire) |
| Meaning 3 | To develop spots on the skin |
| Transitivity | Intransitive |
| Particle Logic | "Out" = movement from confinement |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The prisoners broke out of the jail." (Escape)
- "A fire broke out in the kitchen." (Sudden start)
- "She broke out in hives after eating seafood." (Skin condition)

#### Break Up

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To end a relationship |
| Meaning 2 | To disperse or scatter a crowd/meeting |
| Meaning 3 | To stop a fight |
| Meaning 4 | To begin to laugh or cry suddenly |
| Transitivity | Intransitive/Transitive (separable) |
| Particle Logic | "Up" = completion, separation |

**Usage Examples:**
- "They broke up after five years of dating." (Relationship end)
- "The police broke up the protest." (Disperse)
- "Everyone broke up when the comedian told the joke." (Sudden laughter)

#### Break Off

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To end abruptly (relations, negotiations) |
| Meaning 2 | To separate by breaking |
| Meaning 3 | To stop speaking mid-sentence |
| Transitivity | Transitive separable |
| Particle Logic | "Off" = separation, discontinuation |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The two countries broke off diplomatic relations." (End abruptly)
- "She broke off a piece of chocolate." (Separate by breaking)
- "He broke off mid-sentence when he saw her." (Stop speaking)

### Section 24.4 | The Call Family

The verb *call* combined with particles produces meanings ranging from telecommunications to confrontation.

#### Call Off

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning | To cancel (an event, plan, arrangement) |
| Transitivity | Transitive separable |
| Particle Logic | "Off" = discontinuation, cancellation |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The match was called off due to rain."
- "She called off the meeting at the last minute."

[alert type="warning"]
**Key Distinction:** "Call off" = *cancel*. "Put off" = *postpone*. Candidates frequently confuse these because both involve delaying or stopping an event. "Call off" means permanent cancellation; "put off" means delay to a later time. Examiners test this distinction repeatedly.
[/alert]

**Usage Examples:**

#### Call Out

**Usage Examples:**

#### Call Up

#### Call In

### Section 24.6 | The Come Family

The verb *come* with particles produces meanings related to movement, discovery, and social interaction.

#### Come Across

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To find or encounter by chance |
| Meaning 2 | To be perceived or understood (how someone seems) |
| Meaning 3 | To agree to give (money, information) |
| Transitivity | Transitive inseparable (meaning 1, 3); Intransitive (meaning 2) |
| Particle Logic | "Across" = encounter in one's path |

**Usage Examples:**
- "I came across an old photograph in the drawer." (Find)
- "He comes across as very confident." (Perceived as)
- "She finally came across with the truth." (Reveal)

[alert type="info"]
**Key Distinction:** "Come across" = *encounter by chance* or *perceive*. "Come by" = *acquire* or *visit briefly*. "Come over" = *visit*. These are frequently tested together with subtle differences. "Come across" implies accidental encounter; "come by" implies purposeful acquisition or brief visit; "come over" implies a social visit.
[/alert]

#### Come Out

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To appear; become visible |
| Meaning 2 | To be released (book, film, news) |
| Meaning 3 | To result or end up (in a particular state) |
| Meaning 4 | To declare publicly (homosexuality, position) |
| Transitivity | Intransitive |
| Particle Logic | "Out" = emergence from concealment |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The sun came out after the rain." (Appear)
- "Her new book comes out next month." (Release)
- "The calculations came out correctly." (Result)
- "He came out as gay to his family." (Declare)

#### Come By

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To acquire or obtain (especially with difficulty) |
| Meaning 2 | To visit briefly |
| Transitivity | Transitive inseparable (meaning 1); Intransitive (meaning 2) |
| Particle Logic | "By" = acquisition, brief visit |

**Usage Examples:**
- "How did you come by that rare artifact?" (Acquire)
- "He came by to return the book." (Visit)

#### Come Over

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To visit (someone's home) |
| Meaning 2 | To affect or influence (emotion, feeling) |
| Meaning 3 | To change sides or opinion |
| Transitivity | Intransitive (meaning 1, 2); Transitive inseparable (meaning 3) |
| Particle Logic | "Over" = movement across, visit |

**Usage Examples:**
- "Come over for dinner tonight." (Visit)
- "A feeling of dizziness came over him." (Affect)
- "He came over to our side of the argument." (Change sides)

### Section 24.10 | Solved Examples

[example]
**Question:** The meeting was _____ due to the chairman's sudden illness.
- A) called off
- B) put off
- C) called out
- D) called on

**Solution:** The correct answer is **A) called off**. The sentence indicates cancellation (due to illness). "Put off" (B) means to postpone, not cancel. "Called out" (C) means to shout or criticize. "Called on" (D) means to visit or ask to speak. The context of "due to illness" suggests cancellation rather than postponement. This is a classic trap: candidates who know "put off" as meaning "delay" may choose it incorrectly.
[/example]

[example]
**Question:** The old car finally _____ on the highway, leaving us stranded.
- A) broke up
- B) broke out
- C) broke down
- D) broke off

**Solution:** The correct answer is **C) broke down**. The sentence indicates mechanical failure. "Broke up" (A) means to end a relationship or disperse. "Broke out" (B) means to escape or begin suddenly. "Broke off" (D) means to end abruptly or break a piece. "Break down" specifically refers to mechanical failure (and emotional collapse, but the context "car" clarifies).
[/example]

[example]
**Question:** The scientist _____ the data to find the correlation.
- A) broke down
- B) broke out
- C) broke up
- D) broke through

**Solution:** The correct answer is **A) broke down**. The sentence indicates analysis. "Break down" in its transitive meaning is to analyze into components. "Broke up" (C) means to separate into pieces or end a relationship (not analytical). "Break through" (D) means to make progress or overcome an obstacle. The context of "data" and "correlation" points to analysis.
[/example]

[example]
**Question:** I _____ an old friend at the conference yesterday.
- A) came across
- B) came by
- C) came over
- D) came out

**Solution:** The correct answer is **A) came across**. The sentence indicates accidental encounter. "Came by" (B) means to acquire or visit briefly. "Came over" (C) means to visit a home or be affected by. "Came out" (D) means to appear or be released. "Came across" specifically means to encounter by chance. The word "old friend" and "conference" indicate an unplanned meeting.
[/example]

[example]
**Question:** The company's plans for expansion _____ when the funding was withdrawn.
- A) fell through
- B) fell off
- C) fell apart
- D) fell behind

**Solution:** The correct answer is **A) fell through**. The sentence indicates failure to materialize (plans, with the specific trigger being withdrawn funding). "Fell through" means a plan or deal failed to happen. "Fell off" (B) means to decrease. "Fell apart" (C) means to disintegrate (stronger, implies already started failing). "Fell behind" (D) means to fail to keep up with schedule. The context of "plans for expansion" and "funding withdrawn" specifically indicates failure to happen.
[/example]

### Section 24.12 | The Revision Framework

**The Particle Mapping Strategy:**

When you encounter a phrasal verb question, use the following mental pathway:
1. **Identify the verb.** (Break, call, carry, etc.)
2. **Identify the particle.** (Down, off, on, etc.)
3. **Apply the particle logic.** (Down = reduction/cessation; Off = separation/cancellation; On = continuation/connection)
4. **Confirm from context.**

**The Verb Family Approach:**

Group phrasal verbs by their verb rather than by their particle. This is more effective because the verb provides the semantic anchor, and the particle modifies it. For each verb family, create a mental map:
- **Break:** Down (failure/analysis), Into (entry), Out (escape/start), Up (end/disperse), Off (end abruptly)
- **Call:** Off (cancel), On (visit/ask), Out (shout/criticize), Up (telephone), For (require)
- **Carry:** On (continue), Out (execute), Away (remove/overwhelm), Forward (transfer)
- **Come:** Across (encounter), Out (appear/release), By (acquire/visit), Over (visit/affect)
- **Cut:** Down (reduce), Off (disconnect), Out (remove/stop), Up (chop/criticize)
- **Fall:** Apart (disintegrate), Behind (fail to keep up), For (be deceived/love), Off (decrease), Out (quarrel), Through (fail)
