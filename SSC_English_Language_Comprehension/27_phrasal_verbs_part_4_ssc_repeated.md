## Chapter 27: Phrasal Verbs—Take, Turn, Work & Complete Mastery

### Section 27.1 | The Cognitive Architecture of Final Phrasal Verb Families

The phrasal verbs in this chapter—*take*, *turn*, and *work*—represent the most semantically diverse and contextually rich combinations in the English language. The *take* family alone produces over fifteen distinct meanings across various particles, many of which are tested with remarkable frequency in SSC examinations.

**The Cognitive Rule:** =The verb *take* conveys the core meaning of "acquisition, removal, or assumption." Each particle modifies this core: *up* = assumption/start, *off* = removal/departure, *on* = assumption/employment, *over* = control/acquisition. Understanding this network allows you to navigate the multiple meanings systematically.=

The examination pattern for these families is distinctive: *take* appears in nearly every SSC English paper (often 2-3 questions), *turn* appears in 60% of papers, and *work* appears in 40% of papers. Mastery of this chapter, combined with the previous three, completes your phrasal verb preparation.

[alert type="danger"]

[/alert]

### Section 27.3 | The Turn Family

| Down | Rejection, reduction | Reject, reduce |
| Into | Conversion, transformation | Become, transform into |
| Off | Deactivation, departure | Switch off, repel |
| On | Activation, excitement | Switch on, excite |
| Out | Result, appearance | Result, produce, appear |
| Over | Inversion, transfer | Invert, transfer, consider |
| Up | Arrival, discovery | Arrive, appear, discover |

#### Turn Around

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|

[/alert]

### Section 27.7 | Common Errors: Separable vs. Inseparable Phrasal Verbs

The distinction between separable and inseparable phrasal verbs is a frequent source of errors in examinations. This section provides the definitive framework for understanding this distinction.

#### The Definitive Rule

| Type | Rule | Example | Correct Structure |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Separable | Object can go between verb and particle OR after the particle | "Pick up the book" OR "Pick the book up" | Both are correct |
| Inseparable | Object must follow the particle | "Look after the child" | "Look the child after" is WRONG |
| Pronoun Rule | With separable verbs, pronouns MUST go between verb and particle | "Pick it up" | "Pick up it" is WRONG |
| Three-Part | Always inseparable | "Put up with the noise" | "Put the noise up with" is WRONG |

#### Separable Phrasal Verbs (High-Frequency)

| Phrasal Verb | Correct with Noun | Correct with Pronoun |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Break down (analyze) | Break down the data / Break the data down | Break it down |
| Break up (disperse) | Break up the crowd / Break the crowd up | Break them up |
| Call off (cancel) | Call off the meeting / Call the meeting off | Call it off |
| Carry out (execute) | Carry out the plan / Carry the plan out | Carry it out |
| Cut down (reduce) | Cut down consumption / Cut consumption down | Cut it down |
| Cut off (disconnect) | Cut off the power / Cut the power off | Cut it off |
| Give away (reveal) | Give away the secret / Give the secret away | Give it away |
| Give back (return) | Give back the book / Give the book back | Give it back |
| Give up (quit) | Give up smoking / Give smoking up | Give it up |
| Hold back (restrain) | Hold back the crowd / Hold the crowd back | Hold them back |
| Hold up (delay) | Hold up traffic / Hold traffic up | Hold it up |
| Keep back (withhold) | Keep back the truth / Keep the truth back | Keep it back |
| Look up (search) | Look up the word / Look the word up | Look it up |
| Make up (invent) | Make up a story / Make a story up | Make it up |
| Put off (postpone) | Put off the meeting / Put the meeting off | Put it off |
| Put on (wear) | Put on your coat / Put your coat on | Put it on |
| Put out (extinguish) | Put out the fire / Put the fire out | Put it out |
| Take down (record) | Take down the notes / Take the notes down | Take them down |
| Take off (remove) | Take off your shoes / Take your shoes off | Take them off |
| Take up (begin) | Take up a hobby / Take a hobby up | Take it up |
| Turn down (reject) | Turn down the offer / Turn the offer down | Turn it down |
| Turn off (switch off) | Turn off the light / Turn the light off | Turn it off |
| Turn on (switch on) | Turn on the TV / Turn the TV on | Turn it on |

#### Inseparable Phrasal Verbs (High-Frequency)

| Phrasal Verb | Correct Structure | Incorrect Structure |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Break into | Break into the house | Break the house into |
| Call for | Call for action | Call action for |
| Call on (visit) | Call on the doctor | Call the doctor on |
| Come across | Come across an old friend | Come an old friend across |
| Get over | Get over the illness | Get the illness over |
| Get through (complete) | Get through the work | Get the work through |
| Go after | Go after the job | Go the job after |
| Go over (review) | Go over the notes | Go the notes over |
| Look after | Look after the children | Look the children after |
| Look at | Look at the picture | Look the picture at |
| Look into | Look into the matter | Look the matter into |
| Look for | Look for the keys | Look the keys for |
| Put up with | Put up with the noise | Put the noise up with |
| Run into | Run into an old friend | Run an old friend into |
| Run over (hit) | Run over the dog | Run the dog over (This is separable in meaning 1; see note) |
| Take after | Take after her mother | Take her mother after |
| Take on (assume) | Take on the challenge | Take the challenge on (This is separable—see note) |

[alert type="danger"]
**The Pronoun Exception:** This is the single most frequently tested grammar point in phrasal verb questions. With *separable* phrasal verbs, a pronoun object *must* go between the verb and particle. With *inseparable* phrasal verbs, the pronoun object *must* follow the particle.
- "Pick *it* up" (correct for separable "pick up")
- "Pick up *it*" (WRONG)
- "Look after *them*" (correct for inseparable "look after")
- "Look *them* after" (WRONG)

This distinction appears in nearly 60% of phrasal verb error identification questions. Memorize it absolutely.
[/alert]

#### Exceptions and Special Cases

Some phrasal verbs are separable in one meaning and inseparable in another:

| Phrasal Verb | Meaning 1 (Separable) | Meaning 2 (Inseparable) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Run over | "Run over the dog" OR "Run the dog over" (hit with vehicle) | "Run over the documents" (review quickly) — *inseparable* |
| Take on | "Take on the challenge" OR "Take the challenge on" (assume) | "Take on someone" (compete against) — *inseparable* |
| Get through | "Get through the exam" (complete) — *inseparable* | "Get the message through" (communicate) — *separable* |

[example]
**Question:** Select the grammatically correct sentence:
- A) I look forward to meet you.
- B) I look forward to meeting you.
- C) I look forward meeting you.
- D) I look forward to meet with you.

**Solution:** The correct answer is **B) I look forward to meeting you**. "Look forward to" is a three-part phrasal verb that is always followed by a gerund (-ing form), not an infinitive. Option A uses the infinitive "meet" (incorrect). Option C omits "to" (incorrect). Option D adds "with" unnecessarily (incorrect).
[/example]

[example]
**Question:** Identify the incorrect sentence:
- A) Please pick up the book.
- B) Please pick the book up.
- C) Please pick it up.
- D) Please pick up it.

**Solution:** The correct answer is **D) Please pick up it**. "Pick up" is separable. With a pronoun object ("it"), the pronoun must go between the verb and the particle: "pick it up." "Pick up it" is grammatically incorrect. Options A, B, and C are all correct.
[/example]
