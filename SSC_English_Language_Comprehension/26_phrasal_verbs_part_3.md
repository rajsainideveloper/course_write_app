## Chapter 26: Phrasal Verbs—Lay, Look, Make, Put, Run, Set

### Section 26.1 | The Architecture of Advanced Phrasal Verb Families

The phrasal verbs in this chapter represent the most nuanced and context-sensitive combinations in the English language. The verbs *lay*, *look*, *make*, *put*, *run*, and *set* each produce a constellation of meanings that are frequently tested in Mains-level examinations.

**The Cognitive Rule:** =These phrasal verbs are often tested through *particle selection* questions—the verb is fixed, and you must choose the correct particle from the options. Mastery requires understanding the semantic contribution of each particle to the base verb.=

The examination pattern for these families is distinctive: they appear more frequently in Tier-2/Mains papers than in Prelims. SSC CGL Tier-2 and Bank PO Mains often include 3-4 questions from these families, with a particular emphasis on *put*, *look*, and *set*.

### Section 26.2 | The Lay Family

*Lay* is a transitive verb (it requires an object) combined with particles to produce meanings related to placement, establishment, and dismissal.

#### The Particle Logic for Lay

| Particle | Core Logic | Meaning Cluster |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Down | Placement, establishment | Establish, formulate, surrender |
| Off | Dismissal, cessation | Dismiss (employees), stop |
| Out | Arrangement, planning | Arrange, plan, spend |
| Up | Storage, preservation | Store, accumulate |

[alert type="danger"]
**Critical Distinction:** *Lay* vs. *Lie*. Examiners frequently test this distinction. *Lay* is transitive (requires an object): "Lay the book on the table." *Lie* is intransitive (no object): "I lie down." The confusion occurs because the past tense of *lie* is *lay*—"Yesterday I lay on the couch." The phrasal verbs in this section all use *lay* (transitive). Candidates who confuse the two will make catastrophic errors.
[/alert]

#### Lay Down

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To establish (rules, principles, laws) |
| Meaning 2 | To place (something) on a surface |
| Meaning 3 | To surrender or give up (arms, authority) |
| Transitivity | Transitive separable |
| Particle Logic | "Down" = placement, establishment, surrender |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The committee laid down the new regulations." (Establish)
- "Lay down your weapons and surrender." (Surrender)
- "She laid down the baby in the crib." (Place)

#### Lay Off

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To dismiss employees (temporarily or permanently) |
| Meaning 2 | To stop doing something (informal) |
| Meaning 3 | To leave someone alone |
| Transitivity | Transitive separable |
| Particle Logic | "Off" = cessation, dismissal |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The company laid off 500 workers." (Dismiss)
- "Lay off smoking—it's harmful." (Stop)
- "Lay off him—he's doing his best." (Leave alone)

#### Lay Out

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To arrange or spread out |
| Meaning 2 | To plan or design (how something will be arranged) |
| Meaning 3 | To spend (money, effort) |
| Transitivity | Transitive separable |
| Particle Logic | "Out" = arrangement, planning, expenditure |

**Usage Examples:**
- "She laid out the clothes on the bed." (Arrange)
- "The architect laid out the floor plan." (Design)
- "He laid out a fortune on the car." (Spend)

#### Lay Up

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To store or save for future use |
| Meaning 2 | To disable (especially a ship or vehicle) |
| Meaning 3 | To cause to be confined to bed (by illness) |
| Transitivity | Transitive separable |
| Particle Logic | "Up" = storage, incapacitation |

**Usage Examples:**
- "They laid up supplies for the winter." (Store)
- "The ship was laid up for repairs." (Disable)
- "The injury laid him up for weeks." (Confine to bed)

### Section 26.3 | The Look Family

*Look* combined with particles produces meanings related to visual perception, investigation, and anticipation. This is one of the most frequently tested phrasal verb families in all examinations.

#### The Particle Logic for Look

| Particle | Core Logic | Meaning Cluster |
|:---|:---|:---|
| After | Care, supervision | Take care of, supervise |
| Ahead | Future, anticipation | Plan for the future |
| At | Examination, consideration | Examine, consider |
| Back | Retrospection | Remember, regret |
| Down | Condescension | Disapprove, condescend |
| For | Search, seek | Search for, seek |
| Forward | Anticipation | Eagerly anticipate |
| Into | Investigation | Investigate, examine |
| On | Observation, witnessing | Watch, observe |
| Out | Caution, vigilance | Be careful, beware |

#### Look After

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To take care of (someone, something) |
| Meaning 2 | To ensure the well-being of |
| Transitivity | Transitive inseparable |
| Particle Logic | "After" = care, supervision |

**Usage Examples:**
- "She looks after her elderly parents." (Take care of)
- "Who will look after the children while you're away?" (Supervise)

#### Look Ahead

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning | To think about or plan for the future |
| Transitivity | Intransitive |
| Particle Logic | "Ahead" = future, anticipation |

**Usage Examples:**
- "We need to look ahead and plan for retirement." (Plan for future)
- "Look ahead—don't dwell on the past." (Focus forward)

#### Look At

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To direct one's eyes toward |
| Meaning 2 | To examine or consider |
| Transitivity | Transitive inseparable |
| Particle Logic | "At" = direct attention, examination |

#### Look Back

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|

| Particle Logic | "Back" = retrospection, reversal |

**Usage Examples:**

#### Look Down On

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To consider someone inferior |
| Meaning 2 | To disapprove of or despise |
| Transitivity | Transitive inseparable (three-part) |
| Particle Logic | "Down" = condescension, superiority |

**Usage Examples:**

- "She looks down on gossip and idle talk." (Disapprove)

#### Look For

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To search for or seek |
| Meaning 2 | To expect or anticipate |
| Transitivity | Transitive inseparable |
| Particle Logic | "For" = search, seeking |

**Usage Examples:**
- "I'm looking for my keys." (Search)
- "He's looking for a new job." (Seek)

#### Look Forward To

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To eagerly anticipate (something future) |
| Meaning 2 | To expect with pleasure |
| Transitivity | Transitive inseparable (three-part) |
| Particle Logic | "Forward" = future, anticipation; "To" = object of anticipation |

**Usage Examples:**
- "I look forward to meeting you." (Eagerly anticipate)
- "She looks forward to the holidays every year." (Expect with pleasure)

[alert type="warning"]
**Critical Grammatical Point:** "Look forward to" is followed by a *gerund* (-ing form), not an infinitive. "I look forward to *meeting* you" (correct). "I look forward to *meet* you" (incorrect). This is one of the most frequently tested grammatical errors in SSC and Bank PO examinations. Memorize this rule.
[/alert]

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|

| Particle Logic | "Into" = investigation, internal examination |

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To watch (without participating) |
| Meaning 2 | To regard or consider |
| Transitivity | Intransitive (meaning 1); Transitive inseparable (meaning 2) |
| Particle Logic | "On" = observation, consideration |

### Section 26.4 | The Make Family

*Make* combined with particles produces meanings related to creation, interpretation, and composition.

#### The Particle Logic for Make

| Particle | Core Logic | Meaning Cluster |
|:---|:---|:---|
| For | Direction, contribution | Move toward, contribute to |
| Of | Interpretation, judgment | Interpret, understand |
| Out | Discernment, comprehension | Understand, discern, identify |
| Up | Composition, invention | Invent, compose, reconcile |
| Up for | Compensation | Compensate for |

#### Make For

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To move toward (a place) |
| Meaning 2 | To contribute to (a result, outcome) |
| Meaning 3 | To have a particular effect on |
| Transitivity | Transitive inseparable |
| Particle Logic | "For" = direction, contribution |

**Usage Examples:**
- "The ship made for the harbor." (Move toward)
- "Good communication makes for a happy workplace." (Contribute to)
- "The weather makes for difficult driving conditions." (Result in)

#### Make Of

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To understand or interpret (someone, something) |
| Meaning 2 | To form an opinion about |
| Transitivity | Transitive inseparable |
| Particle Logic | "Of" = interpretation, judgment |

**Usage Examples:**
- "I don't know what to make of his strange behavior." (Interpret)
- "What do you make of the new manager?" (Form opinion)

#### Make Out

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To see or hear with difficulty |
| Meaning 2 | To understand (especially a reason) |
| Meaning 3 | To write out (a document, check) |
| Meaning 4 | To claim or pretend |
| Transitivity | Transitive separable |
| Particle Logic | "Out" = discernment, comprehension |

**Usage Examples:**
- "I can barely make out the distant figures." (See)
- "I can't make out what he's saying." (Hear)
- "She made out a check for $100." (Write)
- "He makes out to be more competent than he is." (Claim)

#### Make Up

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To invent (a story, excuse) |
| Meaning 2 | To compose or constitute |
| Meaning 3 | To reconcile (after a quarrel) |
| Meaning 4 | To apply cosmetics |
| Meaning 5 | To compensate for (something missed) |
| Transitivity | Transitive separable |
| Particle Logic | "Up" = composition, invention, reconciliation |

**Usage Examples:**
- "She made up an excuse for her lateness." (Invent)
- "Women make up 60% of the workforce." (Constitute)
- "They fought and then made up the next day." (Reconcile)
- "She made up her face in the mirror." (Apply cosmetics)
- "I need to make up the work I missed." (Compensate)

#### Make Up For

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning | To compensate for something (deficit, loss, error) |
| Transitivity | Transitive inseparable (three-part) |
| Particle Logic | "Up" + "For" = compensation |

**Usage Examples:**
- "She tried to make up for her mistake." (Compensate)
- "The good reviews made up for the low sales." (Balance out)

[alert type="info"]
**Key Distinction:** "Make up" (reconcile) vs. "Make up for" (compensate). "They made up after their argument" (reconciled). "He tried to make up for the lost time" (compensate). Examiners test whether candidates include the required "for" when compensation is the meaning.
[/alert]

| Phrasal Verb | Meaning | Example |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Make for | Move toward; contribute to | She made for the exit. |
| Make of | Interpret; understand | What do you make of this? |
| Make out | Discern; understand; write | I can make out the sign. |
| Make up | Invent; constitute; reconcile | She made up the story. |
| Make up for | Compensate for | He made up for his absence. |
[/flashcards]

### Section 26.5 | The Put Family

| Particle | Core Logic | Meaning Cluster |

| Away | Storage, disposal | Store, save, discard |
| Down | Placement, suppression | Place, suppress, record |
| Forward | Proposal, advancement | Propose, advance |
| Off | Postponement, discouragement | Postpone, discourage |
| On | Application, activation | Apply, activate, wear |
| Out | Extinction, inconvenience | Extinguish, inconvenience |
| Through | Connection, completion | Connect, complete, endure |
| Up | Accommodation, tolerance | House, tolerate, offer |

#### Put Across

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|

[alert type="danger"]

Examiners test whether you can use the phrasal verb correctly in a sentence. "The meeting was *put off*" is idiomatic. "The meeting was *postponed*" is formal. Both are correct, but the phrasal verb is often tested in error identification questions where candidates incorrectly use the wrong particle.
[/alert]

- "Rain set in after noon." (Begin—weather)
- "Panic set in when they realized the danger." (Begin—feeling)

#### Set Off

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To depart or leave (on a journey) |
| Meaning 2 | To trigger (alarm, bomb, reaction) |
| Meaning 3 | To highlight or enhance (by contrast) |
| Transitivity | Intransitive (meaning 1); Transitive separable (meanings 2, 3) |
| Particle Logic | "Off" = departure, activation, enhancement |

**Usage Examples:**
- "We set off at dawn." (Depart)
- "The smoke set off the fire alarm." (Trigger)
- "The blue dress set off her eyes." (Highlight)

#### Set Up

| Aspect | Detail |
|:---|:---|
| Meaning 1 | To establish or organize (business, system) |
| Meaning 2 | To arrange or install (equipment) |
| Meaning 3 | To trick or frame (someone) |
| Meaning 4 | To provide with (resources) |
| Transitivity | Transitive separable |
| Particle Logic | "Up" = establishment, arrangement, deception |

**Usage Examples:**
- "They set up a new company." (Establish)
- "Help me set up the equipment." (Arrange)
- "He was set up by his enemies." (Trick)
- "Her parents set her up in business." (Provide with)

### Section 26.9 | Solved Examples

[example]
**Question:** The police are _____ the matter of the missing documents.
- A) looking after
- B) looking at
- C) looking into
- D) looking over

**Solution:** The correct answer is **C) looking into**. The sentence indicates investigation. "Look into" means to investigate or examine. "Look after" (A) means to take care of. "Look at" (B) means to direct one's eyes or examine superficially. "Look over" (D) means to review quickly. The context of "matter" and "missing documents" specifically requires the investigative meaning of "look into."
[/example]

[example]
**Question:** The company _____ 500 employees last month due to budget cuts.
- A) laid down
- B) laid out
- C) laid off
- D) laid up

**Solution:** The correct answer is **C) laid off**. The sentence indicates dismissal of employees. "Lay off" means to dismiss employees. "Lay down" (A) means to establish or place. "Lay out" (B) means to arrange or plan. "Lay up" (D) means to store or confine to bed. The context of "employees" and "budget cuts" specifically requires "lay off."
[/example]

[example]
**Question:** I had to _____ all the reasons for my decision in the report.
- A) set up
- B) set down
- C) set aside
- D) set off

**Solution:** The correct answer is **B) set down**. The sentence indicates recording in writing. "Set down" means to record in writing. "Set up" (A) means to establish or arrange. "Set aside" (C) means to reserve or disregard. "Set off" (D) means to depart or trigger. The context of "reasons" and "report" indicates writing them down.
[/example]

[example]
**Question:** The economic crisis _____ the country's development by decades.
- A) set back
- B) set aside
- C) set down
- D) set about

**Solution:** The correct answer is **A) set back**. The sentence indicates hindrance or delay. "Set back" means to hinder or delay. "Set aside" (B) means to reserve or disregard. "Set down" (C) means to place or record. "Set about" (D) means to begin. The context of "economic crisis" and "development" indicates a negative impact that delayed progress.
[/example]

[example]
**Question:** She tried to _____ her absence by working extra hours.
- A) make up
- B) make up for
- C) make out
- D) make of

**Solution:** The correct answer is **B) make up for**. The sentence indicates compensation. "Make up for" means to compensate for something. "Make up" (A) can mean to invent, constitute, or reconcile—but without "for" it does not mean compensate. "Make out" (C) means to discern or understand. "Make of" (D) means to interpret. The context of "absence" and "working extra hours" specifically requires "make up for."
[/example]
