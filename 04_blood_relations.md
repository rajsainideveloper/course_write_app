Blood RelationsThe Geometry of Kinship: Mapping Human Connections Through Logical InferenceChapter 4: The Complete Logic of Blood RelationsSection 1 | The Foundational Architecture of Kinship MappingBlood relation problems are, at their core, exercises in graph theory applied to family structures. You are constructing a directed graph where nodes represent individuals and edges represent relationships. The challenge lies in the fact that the graph is implicit—you must infer its structure from textual or symbolic clues.The fundamental unit of analysis is the nuclear family:Parents: Father (F) and Mother (M)Children: Son (S) and Daughter (D)Siblings: Brother (B) and Sister (Si)From these atomic units, all complex relationships—grandparents, uncles, aunts, cousins, nephews, nieces—are derived through composition.The First Principle of Blood Relations:The gender of an individual is the single most critical piece of information. Without gender, you cannot determine whether someone is a father, mother, brother, or sister. The exam writers will often omit gender explicitly, forcing you to infer it from the relationship (e.g., "A is the son of B" establishes A's gender as male).[alert type="danger"]The Gender Neutrality Trap:Terms like parent, child, sibling, and cousin are gender-neutral. When you encounter these, you must pause and consider both possibilities. Many students make fatal errors by assuming a gender that is not explicitly stated.[/alert]The Standard Notation System:Male: Use a square or a single line. Represent with M or □.Female: Use a circle or a double line. Represent with F or ○.Marriage: Represented by a horizontal line ---.Parent-Child: Represented by a vertical line |.Siblings: Represented by a horizontal line --- connected at the top.[flashcards title="Core Kinship Terminology"]TermDefinition=Father=Male parent=Mother=Female parent=Son=Male child=Daughter=Female child=Brother=Male sibling=Sister=Female sibling=Spouse=Husband or Wife=Sibling=Brother or Sister (gender-neutral)[/flashcards]Section 2 | Direct Relation (Mother/Father/Son/Daughter)These are the simplest relationships, but they form the building blocks of all complex problems.
